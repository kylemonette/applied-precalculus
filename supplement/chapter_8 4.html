    A man takes \(3\) seconds to breathe in and out once. The volume of air in his lungs, \(V(t)\text{,}\) varies between a minimum of \(0.5\) liters and a maximum of \(4.5\) liters. Assume that at time \(t=0\) the volume is at a minimum; that is, time \(t=0\) is when the man begins to breathe in.

    What are the period, amplitude, and midline of \(V(t)\text{?}\)

    The midline equation is
    \begin{equation*} y \quad = \quad \frac{y_\text{max} + y_\text{min} }{2} \quad = \quad \frac{4.5+0.5}{2} \quad = \quad 2.5. \end{equation*}

    \(t\) \(0\) \(1\) \(2\) \(3\) \(4\) \(5\) \(6\)

    1.

    Determine the radian measure of each angle and in which quadrant the terminal side of the angle is located.
    🔗
    (a)
    \(12^\circ\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Multiply by \(\frac{\pi}{180}\) to convert:
    \begin{equation*} 12^\circ \cdot \frac{\pi}{180} \quad = \quad \frac{12\pi}{180} \quad = \quad \frac{\pi}{15} \rad . \end{equation*}
    This is in quadrant 1.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    \(450^\circ\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Multiply by \(\frac{\pi}{180}\) to convert:
    \begin{equation*} 450^\circ \cdot \frac{\pi}{180} \quad = \quad \frac{450\pi }{180} \quad = \quad \frac{5\pi}{2} \rad. \end{equation*}
    Note that \(\frac{5\pi}{2}\) radians is coterminal to \(\frac{\pi}{2}\) radians which is \(90^\circ \text{,}\) so this angle is on the positive \(y\)-axis.

    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    \(610^\circ\)

    Solution.

    \begin{equation*} 610^\circ \cdot \frac{\pi}{180} \quad = \quad \frac{610\pi }{180} \quad = \quad \frac{61\pi }{18} \rad. \end{equation*}
    This is in quadrant 3, because it is coterminal to \(250^\circ \text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    \(-275^\circ\)

    Solution.

    \(45^\circ\)
    🔗

    \begin{equation*} S = \frac{\pi}{4}\cdot 3 = \frac{3\pi}{4} \text{ feet} . \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    \(\dfrac{3\pi}{4}\)
    🔗

    \begin{equation*} S = \frac{3\pi}{4}\cdot 3 = \frac{9\pi}{4} \text{ feet} . \end{equation*}

    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    \(2\pi\)
    🔗
    Solution.

    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    \(360^\circ\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Because \(360^\circ = 2\pi\) radians, then the arc length is
    \begin{equation*} S = 2\pi \cdot 3 = 6\pi \text{ feet} . \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (e)
    \(\dfrac{\pi}{4}\)
    🔗
    Solution.

    1.

    A rabbit population, \(R(t)\text{,}\) in a national park rises and falls each year. It is at its minimum of \(4000\) rabbits in January. By July, as the weather warms up and food grows more abundant, the population triples in size to \(12000\text{.}\) But the following January, the population again falls to \(4000\) rabbits, completing the cycle.
    🔗
    (a)
    What are the period, amplitude, and midline of \(R(t)\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The period is \(12\) months. The amplitude is
    \begin{equation*} \text{Amp.} \quad = \quad \frac{y_\text{max} - y_\text{min} }{2} \quad = \quad \frac{12000-4000}{2} \quad=\quad 4000 \quad \text{ rabbits} . \end{equation*}
    The midline equation is
    \begin{equation*} y \quad=\quad \frac{y_\text{max} + y_\text{min} }{2} \quad = \quad \frac{12000 + 4000}{2} \quad = \quad 8000. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Find a possible formula for \(R(t)\text{.}\) Graph two full cycles of the function starting with \(t=0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Note that \(R(t)\) is a negative cosine function because the vertical intercept (when \(t=0\)) is a minimum in the cycle. That is,
    \begin{equation*} A = -4000, \qquad B = \frac{2\pi}{P} = \frac{2\pi}{12} = \frac{\pi}{6}, \qquad C = 8000. \end{equation*}
    Therefore the function is
    \begin{equation*} R(t) = -4000 \cos \pbr{\frac{\pi}{6}t} + 8000. \end{equation*}
    Its graph is shown below.

    4.

    Find a possible formula for the following periodic functions.
    🔗
    (a)
    Solution.
    The amplitude is
    \begin{equation*} \text{Amp.} \quad = \quad \frac{y_\text{max} - y_\text{min} }{2} \quad = \quad \frac{6-2}{2} \quad = \quad 2. \end{equation*}
    The period is \(P = 30\pi \) and so
    \begin{equation*} B \quad = \quad \frac{2\pi}{P} \quad = \quad \frac{2\pi}{30\pi } \quad = \quad \frac{1}{15}. \end{equation*}
    The midline equation is
    \begin{equation*} y \quad = \quad \frac{y_\text{max} + y_\text{min} }{2} \quad = \quad \frac{6+2}{2} \quad = \quad 4. \end{equation*}
    This is a negative sine function because the vertical intercept is on the midline and then the function decreases. Therefore
    \begin{equation*} y = -2\sin \pbr{\frac{1}{15}x} + 4. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Solution.

    The period is \(P = 16\pi \) and so
    \begin{equation*} B \quad = \quad \frac{2\pi}{P} \quad = \quad \frac{2\pi}{16\pi } \quad = \quad \frac{1}{8}. \end{equation*}
    The midline equation is
    \begin{equation*} y \quad = \quad \frac{y_\text{max} + y_\text{min} }{2} \quad = \quad \frac{9+3}{2} \quad = \quad 6. \end{equation*}
    This is a negative sine function because the vertical intercept is on the midline and then the function decreases. Therefore
    \begin{equation*} y = -3\sin \pbr{\frac{1}{8}x} + 6. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    Solution.

    \begin{equation*} \text{Amp.} \quad = \quad \frac{y_\text{max} - y_\text{min} }{2} \quad = \quad \frac{7-(-7)}{2} \quad = \quad 7. \end{equation*}
    The period is \(P = 10 \) and so
    \begin{equation*} B \quad = \quad \frac{2\pi}{P} \quad = \quad \frac{2\pi}{10} \quad = \quad \frac{\pi }{5}. \end{equation*}
    The midline equation is
    \begin{equation*} y \quad = \quad \frac{y_\text{max} + y_\text{min} }{2} \quad = \quad \frac{7+(-7)}{2} \quad = \quad 0. \end{equation*}
    This is a negative sine function because the vertical intercept is on the midline and then the function decreases. Therefore
    \begin{equation*} y = -7\sin \pbr{\frac{\pi }{5}t}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    Solution.
    The amplitude is
    \begin{equation*} \text{Amp.} \quad = \quad \frac{y_\text{max} - y_\text{min} }{2} \quad = \quad \frac{3-(-3)}{2} \quad = \quad 3. \end{equation*}
    The period is \(P = 18\) and so
    \begin{equation*} B \quad = \quad \frac{2\pi}{P} \quad = \quad \frac{2\pi}{18} \quad = \quad \frac{\pi }{9}. \end{equation*}
    The midline equation is
    \begin{equation*} y \quad = \quad \frac{y_\text{max} + y_\text{min} }{2} \quad = \quad \frac{3+(-3)}{2} \quad = \quad 0. \end{equation*}
    This is a positive sine function because the vertical intercept is on the midline and then the function increases. Therefore
    \begin{equation*} y = 3 \sin \pbr{\frac{\pi }{9}t}. \end{equation*}

    5.

    The following table shows values of a periodic function \(f(x)\text{.}\) The maximum value attained by \(f(x)\) is 4.
    🔗
    \(x\) \(0\) \(5\) \(10\) \(15\) \(20\) \(25\) \(30\)
    \(f(x)\) \(4\) \(0\) \(-4\) \(0\) \(4\) \(0\) \(-4\)
    Find the period, amplitude, and midline for \(f(x)\text{.}\) Then find a possible formula for \(f(x)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The period is \(P=20\) and so \(B = \frac{2\pi}{P} = \frac{2\pi}{20} = \frac{\pi}{10}\text{.}\) The amplitude is
    \begin{equation*} \text{Amp.} \quad = \quad \frac{y_\text{max} - y_\text{min} }{2} \quad = \quad \frac{4-(-4)}{2} \quad = \quad 4. \end{equation*}
    The midline equation is
    \begin{equation*} y \quad = \quad \frac{y_\text{max} + y_\text{min} }{2} \quad = \quad \frac{4+(-4)}{2} \quad = \quad 0. \end{equation*}
    Note that this must be a cosine function because the vertical intercept \((0,4)\) is a maximum output in the cycle. Therefore
    \begin{equation*} y = 4 \cos \pbr{\frac{\pi}{10}x}. \end{equation*}
