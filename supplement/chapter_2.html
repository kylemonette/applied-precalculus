    2.

    The rental charge, \(C\text{,}\) at a video store is given by \(C=4.29+3.99n\text{,}\) where \(n\) is the number of days greater than \(2\) for which the video is kept. Give the initial value and the slope and explain their meaning in practical terms.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The initial value (vertical intercept) is \(4.29\text{.}\) Therefore, the cost to rent from the video store for only \(2\) days is \(\$4.29\text{.}\) The slope is \(3.99\text{.}\) Therefore, the cost to rent from the video store goes up by \(\$3.99\) for each day over \(2\) days.
    🔗

    3.

    The number of people, \(P\text{,}\) remaining in the lecture hall \(s\) seconds after the MTH 103 lecture has ended is given by \(P=75-5s\text{.}\) Give the initial value and the slope and explain their meaning in practical terms.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The initial value (vertical intercept) is \(75\text{.}\) Thus, there are \(75\) people attending lecture. The slope is \(-5\text{.}\) Thus, the number of people remaining in the lecture hall goes down by \(5\) people for each second after the end of lecture.

    4.

    Find a formula for the linear function \(f(x)\) if \(f(-10)=15\) and \(f(15)=-20\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Use the ordered pairs \((-10,15)\) and \((15,-20)\text{.}\) Then
    \begin{equation*} m = \frac{-20-15}{15-(-10)} = \frac{-35}{25} = -\frac{7}{5}. \end{equation*}
    Plugging into \(f(x)=mx+b\text{,}\) then
    \begin{equation*} f(x) = -\frac{7}{5}x + b. \end{equation*}
    Using any ordered pair, say \((-10,15)\text{,}\) then
    \begin{equation*} 15=-\frac{7}{5} (-10) + b \quad \implies \quad 15=14+b \quad \implies \quad b =1. \end{equation*}
    Therefore \(f(x) = -\dfrac{7}{5}x+1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    Find a formula for the linear function \(g(x)\) if \(g(2)=6\) and \(g(4)=14\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Use the ordered pairs \((2,6)\) and \((4,14)\text{.}\) Then
    \begin{equation*} m = \frac{14-6}{4-2} = \frac{8}{2}=4. \end{equation*}
    Plugging into \(g(x)=mx+b\text{,}\) then
    \begin{equation*} g(x) = 4x+b. \end{equation*}
    Using any ordered pair, say \((2,6)\text{,}\) then
    \begin{equation*} 6=4(2) + b \quad \implies \quad 6 = 8 + b \quad \implies \quad b = -2. \end{equation*}
    Therefore \(g(x)=4x-2\text{.}\)
    🔗

    6.

    Find an equation of the line passing through \((-1,5)\) and \((0,5)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Note that \((0,5)\) gives us the vertical intercept already, so \(b=5\text{.}\) The slope is
    \begin{equation*} m = \frac{5-5}{0-(-1)} = \frac{0}{1}=0. \end{equation*}
    Therefore the equation is \(y=0x + 5\) or simply \(y=5\text{.}\)

    1.

    Determine whether each equation has one solution, no solutions, or an infinite number of solutions. If there is a solution, find it.
    🔗
    (a)
    \(3(x-1)=-3(x+1)\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Distributing on each side,
    \begin{equation*} 3x-3 = -3x-3. \end{equation*}
    Adding \(3\) to both sides,
    \begin{equation*} 3x=-3x. \end{equation*}
    Adding \(3x\) to both sides,
    \begin{equation*} 6x=0. \end{equation*}
    Therefore \(x=0\) is the only solution.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    \(6x+5=-4\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Subtracting \(5\) from both sides,
    \begin{equation*} 6x=-9. \end{equation*}
    Dividing both sides by \(6\text{,}\)
    \begin{equation*} x = -\frac{9}{6} = -\frac{3}{2} \end{equation*}
    is the only solution.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    \(5x+7=5(x+1)+1\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Distributing on the right side,
    \begin{equation*} 5x+7 = 5x+5+1 . \end{equation*}
    Therefore we have
    \begin{equation*} 5x+7 = 5x+6. \end{equation*}
    Subtracting \(5x\) from both sides,
    \begin{equation*} 7=6. \end{equation*}
    Clearly impossible!! So there is no solution.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    \(-2x+3=-2(x-1)+1\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Distributing on the right side,
    \begin{equation*} -2x+3 = -2x+2+1. \end{equation*}
    Therefore we have
    \begin{equation*} -2x +3 = -2x+3. \end{equation*}
    Adding \(2x\) to both sides,
    \begin{equation*} 3=3. \end{equation*}
    Clearly always true!! Therefore there are infinitely many solutions.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Solve the equation \(g(t)=5\) where \(g(t)=\dfrac{-7}{6-2t}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    We set up
    \begin{equation*} 5 = \frac{-7}{6-2t}. \end{equation*}
    Multiplying both sides by \(6-2t\text{,}\) then
    \begin{equation*} 5(6-2t) = -7. \end{equation*}
    Distributing on the left,
    \begin{equation*} 30 - 10t = -7 \end{equation*}
    and then subtracting \(30\) from both sides,
    \begin{equation*} -10t = -37 \quad \implies \quad t = \frac{-37}{-10} = 3.7. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Is each line horizontal, vertical, or neither? If it is horizontal or vertical, write an equation for the line and graph the line.
    🔗
    (a)
    The line through \((0,6)\) and \((1,6)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The slope is given by
    \begin{equation*} m = \frac{6-6}{1-0} = \frac{0}{1}=0. \end{equation*}
    Therefore the line is horizontal, and further the vertical intercept was given as \(b=6\) so \(y=6\) is the equation. The graph is below.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    The line through \((1,3)\) and \((-4,1)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The slope is
    \begin{equation*} m = \frac{1-3}{-4-1} = \frac{-2}{-5} = \frac{2}{5}. \end{equation*}
    So the line is neither vertical nor horizontal.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    The line through \((2,-5)\) and \((2,4)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The slope is
    \begin{equation*} m = \frac{4-(-5)}{2-2} = \frac{9}{0} \qquad \text{which is undefined!} \end{equation*}
    Therefore the line is vertical, and the \(x\)-intercept is \(x=2\text{.}\) The graph is below.

    Numerically Given Linear Functions.

    Decide if the given table could represent a linear function and explain your reasoning. For each table that could represent a linear function, find a formula for that function.
    🔗
    1.
    \(x\) \(0\) \(1\) \(2\) \(3\)
    \(y\) \(12.6\) \(9.3\) \(6.0\) \(2.7\)
    Solution.
    Notice that \(x\) changes by \(+1\) moving left to right, and \(y\) changes by \(-3.3\text{.}\) Therefore, the slope between each pair of consecutive points is always \(\frac{-3.3}{1} =-3.3\text{,}\) and because this is the same number for all, this is a linear function. Its slope is \(-3.3\) and its vertical intercept is \(12.6\) (from the table) so
    \begin{equation*} y = -3.3x + 12.6. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    2.
    \(t\) \(-1\) \(1\) \(3\) \(5\)
    \(f(t)\) \(0.1\) \(0.4\) \(0.7\) \(1.0\)
    Solution.
    The change in \(t\) (the “run”) is always \(2\text{,}\) and the change in \(f(t)\) (the “rise”) is always \(0.3\text{,}\) and so this is a linear function with slope \(m=\frac{0.3}{2} = 0.15\text{.}\)
    🔗
    To find the equation, either use point-slope form which would look something like
    \begin{equation*} f(t)=0.15(t-(-1))+0.1 = 0.15(t+1)+0.1 \end{equation*}
    or use slope-intercept form and solve for \(b\text{.}\) That is,
    \begin{equation*} f(-1) = 0.1 = 0.15(-1) + b \quad \implies \quad b = 0.25 \end{equation*}
    and so \(f(t)=0.15t + 0.25\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    3.
    \(n\) \(0\) \(1.5\) \(3\) \(4.5\) \(6\)
    \(g(n)\) \(12\) \(18\) \(12\) \(6\) \(12\)
    Solution.
    The change in \(n\) (the “run”) is always \(1.5\text{,}\) but the change in \(g(n)\) (the “rise”) changes — it is \(6\text{,}\) \(-6\text{,}\) \(-6\text{,}\) and then \(6\text{.}\) Because these are not always the same number, the slopes between consecutive points are not equal, and so the function is nonlinear.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    4.
    \(t\) \(0\) \(2\) \(4\) \(6\) \(8\)
    \(h(t)\) \(6.4\) \(1.6\) \(0.4\) \(0.1\) \(0.025\)
    Solution.
    The change in \(t\) (the “run”) is always \(2\text{,}\) but the change in \(h(t)\) (the “rise”) changes; it is \(-4.4\text{,}\) \(-1.2\text{,}\) \(-0.3\text{,}\) and then \(-0.0075\text{.}\) Because these are not always the same number, the slopes between consecutive points are not equal, and so the function is nonlinear.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    5.
    \(z\) \(-2\) \(1\) \(4\) \(7\)
    \(w(z)\) \(6\) \(4.8\) \(3.6\) \(2.4\)
    Solution.
    The change in \(z\) is always \(3\text{,}\) and the change in \(w(z)\) is always \(-1.2\) and so this is a linear function. The slope is \(m = \frac{-1.2}{3} = -0.4\text{,}\) and we can find the equation using point-slope form such as
    \begin{equation*} w(z) = -0.4(z-(-2)) + 6 = -0.4(z+2)+6 \end{equation*}
    or use slope-intercept form and solve for \(b\text{,}\) such as
    \begin{equation*} w(z) = -0.4z + b \quad \implies \quad 6 = -0.4(-2) + b \quad \implies \quad b = 5.2. \end{equation*}
    And therefore \(w(z) = -0.4z + 5.2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    6.

    The height of a human individual can be estimated by the length of the femur, as shown for males in the following table.
    🔗
    \(L\) (length of femur in cm) \(42\) \(45\) \(48\) \(51\)
    \(H\) (height of human male in cm) \(162.97\) \(169.93\) \(176.89\) \(183.85\)
    (a)
    Use the information in this table to find a possible formula for the height of a human male \(H\) as a function of his femur length \(L\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Note that the change in \(L\) is \(3\text{,}\) and the change in \(H\) is always \(6.96\) and so this is a linear function with slope \(m = \frac{6.96}{3} = 2.32\text{.}\) Now, either use point-slope form or slope-intercept form to obtain the equation for the function. Using slope-intercept form, then
    \begin{equation*} H = 2.32L + b. \end{equation*}
    Plugging in any point, say \((42,162.97)\text{,}\) results in
    \begin{equation*} 162.97 = 2.32 (42) + b \end{equation*}
    meaning that \(b = 65.53\) and so
    \begin{equation*} H = 2.32L + 65.53. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    What would the approximate height of a human male with femur length \(46\) cm be?
    🔗
    Solution.
    By plugging in \(L = 46\text{,}\) then the approximate height is
    \begin{equation*} H = 2.32(46) + 65.53 = 172.23 \text{ cm} . \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    Fernando is \(175.4\) cm tall. What would you expect the approximate length of his femur to be?
    🔗
    Solution.
    With a height of \(H = 175.4\text{,}\) then
    \begin{equation*} 175.4 = 2.32L + 65.53. \end{equation*}
    Solving for \(L\text{,}\) we get
    \begin{equation*} 109.87 = 2.32 L \quad \implies \quad L = 47.36 \text{ cm} . \end{equation*}
