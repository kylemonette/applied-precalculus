    Simplifying Expressions.

    Simplify the following so that positive exponents remain.
    🔗
    1.
    \((x^2y^5)^0\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    This expression equals \(1\text{,}\) since anything to the power \(0\) is just \(1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    2.
    \(\pbr{\dfrac{x^{-3}}{xy^2}}^{-1}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{align*} \pbr{\dfrac{x^{-3}}{xy^2}}^{-1} \amp \quad = \quad \pbr{\frac{x^{-3-1}}{y^2} } ^{-1}\\ \amp \quad = \quad \pbr{\frac{x^{-4}}{y^2}}^{-1}\\ \amp\quad = \quad \frac{y^2}{x^{-4}}\\ \amp \quad = \quad y^2x^4 \end{align*}
    🔗
    There are other avenues to take to get to this answer. You could have distributed the \(-1\) power to the numerator and denominator first, for example.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    3.
    \(\dfrac{(x-y)^3}{x}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    This cannot be simplified any further using exponent rules.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    4.
    \((2x^2y^4)^3\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} (2x^2y^4)^3 \quad=\quad 2^3(x^2)^3(y^4)^3 \quad = \quad 8 x^{2\cdot 3} y^{4\cdot 3} \quad = \quad 8 x^6 y^{12}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    5.
    \(\dfrac{x+y}{y}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    This cannot be simplified further using exponent rules. Namely, be sure not to cancel the \(y\) from the numerator!
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    6.
    \(\pbr{\dfrac{\pi (x^{2})^6}{x^{2}y^{-4}}}^2\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{align*} \pbr{\dfrac{\pi (x^{2})^6}{x^{2}y^{-4}}}^2 \amp \quad = \quad \pbr{\frac{\pi x^{12}}{x^2y^{-4}} } ^{2}\\ \amp \quad = \quad \pbr{\frac{\pi x^{12-2}}{y^{-4}} } ^{2}\\ \amp \quad = \quad \pbr{\frac{\pi x^{10}}{y^{-4}} } ^2\\ \amp \quad = \quad \pbr{\pi x^{10}y^{4}} ^2\\ \amp \quad = \quad \pi ^2 \cdot (x^{10})^2 \cdot (y^4)^2\\ \amp \quad = \quad \pi ^2 x^{20} y^{8} \end{align*}
    🔗
    There are other avenues to take to get to this answer. You could have distributed the \(2\) power to the numerator and denominator first, for example.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    7.
    \(\dfrac{x^2+y^3}{xy}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    This cannot be simplified further using exponent rules.
    🔗

    7.
    \(y=\dfrac{14x^{-2}}{21\sqrt{x^7}}\)
    🔗

    \begin{equation*} y \quad = \quad \frac{2 x^{-2}}{3x^{7 /2}} \quad = \quad \frac{2}{3} x^{-2-7 /2} \quad = \quad \frac{2}{3}x^{-11 /2}. \end{equation*}

    Solving Equations.

    Solve each equation. When there is no real solution, state that to be the case.
    🔗
    9.
    \(a^{1/3}-3=5\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Adding \(3\) to both sides, \(a^{1 /3} = 8\text{.}\) Cubing each side (raise to the third power), then
    \begin{equation*} \pbr{a^{1 /3}} ^3 = 8^3 \quad \implies \quad a = 8^3 = 512. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    10.
    \(1+\sqrt{2x+3}=6\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Subtracting \(1\text{,}\) then \(\sqrt{2x+3} = 5\text{.}\) Squaring both sides,
    \begin{equation*} \pbr{\sqrt{2x+3}}^2 = 5^2 \quad \implies \quad 2x+3 = 25 \quad \implies \quad 2x=22 \quad \implies \quad x=11 . \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    11.
    \(7(b+2)^3-26=30\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Adding \(26\) to both sides, then \(7(b+2)^3 = 56\text{.}\) Divide both sides to \(7\) to get \((b+2)^3 = 8\text{.}\) Then, we take the cube root:
    \begin{equation*} \sqrt[3]{(b+2)^3} = \sqrt[3]{8} \quad \implies \quad b+2 = 2 \quad \implies \quad b=0 . \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    12.
    \(2y^6+8=0\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Subtracting \(8\) from both sides, then \(2y^6 = -8\text{.}\) Dividing by \(2\text{,}\) then \(y^6 = -4\text{.}\) Note that this equation does not have a solution because we cannot take an even root (namely, the \(6\)th root) of a negative number.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    13.
    \(80=100+10x^5\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Subtracting \(100\text{,}\) then \(-20 = 10x^5\text{.}\) Dividing by \(10\) yields \(-2 = x^5\text{.}\) We can take the \(5\)th root here, \(5\) being an odd number. So
    \begin{equation*} \sqrt[5]{-2} = \sqrt[5]{x^5} \quad \implies \quad \sqrt[5]{-2} = x . \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    14.
    \((3w-5)^4+4=20\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Subtracting \(4\text{,}\) then \((3w-5)^4 = 16\text{.}\) Taking the \(4\)th root of both sides yields
    \begin{equation*} 3w-5 = \pm \sqrt[4]{16} \quad \implies \quad 3w-5=\pm 2. \end{equation*}
    One solution is when \(3w-5=2\text{,}\) which means \(3w=7\) so \(w=\frac{7}{3}\text{.}\) The second solution is when \(3w-5=-2\text{,}\) which means \(3w=3\) so \(w=1\text{.}\) Hence the two solutions are \(w=\frac{7}{3}\) and \(w=1\text{.}\)

    Power Functions.

    Identify the exponent and the coefficient of each power function.
    🔗
    1.
    \(y=2x^2\cdot4x^3\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Simplifying we get \(y=8 x^{2+3} = 8x^5\text{.}\) So the exponent is \(5\) and the coefficient is \(8\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    2.
    \(y=(x^2)^3\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Simplifying we get \(y=x^{2\cdot 3} = x^6\text{.}\) So the exponent is \(6\) and the coefficient is \(1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    3.
    \(y=\dfrac{x^8}{7x^3}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Simplifying we get \(y=\dfrac{x^{8-3}}{7} = \dfrac{x^5}{7}\) which we can write as \(y=\frac{1}{7}x^5\text{.}\) Therefore the coefficient is \(\frac{1}{7}\) and the exponent is \(5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    4.
    \(y=(0.1x^2)^5(-4x)^2\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{align*} y \amp \quad = \quad (0.1)^5 (x^2)^5 (-4)^2 (x)^2\\ \amp \quad = \quad 0.00001 \,x^{10} (16) x^2\\ \amp \quad = \quad 0.00016 \,x^{10+2}\\ \amp \quad = \quad 0.00016 \,x^{12} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Therefore the coefficient is \(0.00016\) and the exponent is \(12\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    5.
    \(y=\pbr{\dfrac{(2x)^4}{x^3}}^2\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{align*} y\amp \quad = \quad \pbr{\frac{16 x^4}{x^3} } ^2\\ \amp \quad = \quad \pbr{16 x^{4-3}} ^2\\ \amp \quad = \quad \pbr{16x} ^2\\ \amp \quad = \quad 16^2 \cdot x^2\\ \amp \quad = \quad 256 \cdot x^2 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Therefore the coefficient is \(256\) and the exponent is \(2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    6.
    \(y=\dfrac{8x}{\pi^2}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Here, \(y=\dfrac{8}{\pi ^2} \cdot x\) and so the coefficient is \(\dfrac{8}{\pi ^2}\) and the exponent is \(1\text{.}\)

    Power Functions.

    Identify the exponent and the coefficient of each power function.
    🔗
    1.
    \(y=\dfrac{7}{2x^3}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    We have \(y=\frac{7}{2}x^{-3}\) so the coefficient is \(\frac{7}{2}\) and the exponent is \(-3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    2.
    \(y=6\sqrt[3]{x}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    We have \(y=6 x^{1 /3}\) so the coefficient is \(6\) and the exponent is \(1 /3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    3.
    \(y=\dfrac{25x^4}{125x^7}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Note that \(\frac{25}{125}\) reduces to \(\frac{1}{5}\text{.}\) Therefore
    \begin{equation*} y \quad = \quad \frac{1}{5} x^{4-7} \quad = \quad \frac{1}{5}x^{-3}. \end{equation*}
    So the coefficient is \(\frac{1}{5}\) and the exponent is \(-3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    4.
    \(y=\dfrac{2x^4}{3\sqrt{x}}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} y \quad = \quad \frac{2x^{4}}{3x^{1 /2}} \quad = \quad \frac{2}{3}x^{4 - 1/2} \quad = \quad \frac{2}{3}x^{7 /2}. \end{equation*}
    So the coefficient is \(\frac{2}{3}\) and the exponent is \(\frac{7}{2}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    5.
    \(y=(5x^2)^{-2}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Here we have \(y=(5)^{-2} \cdot (x^2)^{-2} = \frac{1}{5^2} \cdot x^{-4} = \frac{1}{25}x^{-4}\text{.}\) So the coefficient is \(\frac{1}{25}\) and the exponent is \(-4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    6.
    \(y=(0.5x^{-3})^2(5x^6)^3\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} y \quad = \quad (0.5)^2 (x^{-3})^2 (5)^3 (x^6)^3 \quad = \quad \frac{1}{4} \cdot x^{-6} \cdot 125 \cdot x^{18} \quad = \quad \frac{125}{4} \cdot x^{12}. \end{equation*}
    Therefore the coefficient is \(\frac{125}{4}\) and the exponent is \(12\text{.}\)
