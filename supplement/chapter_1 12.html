    1.

    A caffeine addict has \(\$20\) and buys \(c\) cups of coffee at \(\$1.25\) per cup. The amount of money that he has left depends on the number of cups that he buys.
    🔗
    (a)
    Why is this scenario an example of a function? What is the input? What is the output?
    🔗
    Solution.
    This is a function because if they purchase a specific number of cups of coffee, there is exactly one amount of money left. The input is the number of cups of coffee purchased, the output is the amount of money left.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Write an equation/formula expressing the amount of money \(D\) that the caffeine addict will have left after purchasing \(c\) cups of coffee.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(D = 20 - 1.25 c\text{.}\) (They lose \(\$1.25\) from their \(\$20\) for each cup of coffee \(c\) they buy)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    What is the domain? What is the range?
    🔗
    Solution.
    The domain is the set of all possible numbers of cups of coffee they could purchase, which are integers (whole numbers) and specifically must be between \(0\) and \(16\text{.}\) The \(16\) is there because it is the maximum number of cups of coffee they can purchase with \(\$20\). The range is the set of all possible amounts of money they could have left from the initial \(\$20\text{.}\) So numbers from \(0\) to \(20\) spaced by \(1.25\text{.}\)
    🔗

    3.

    Write an expression for the total cost of an item of sticker price \(\$p\) that is on sale for \(\$5\) off given that the sales tax is \(7\%\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The total cost is equal to the sale price plus the sales tax on the sale price. That is,
    \begin{equation*} \text{total cost } \quad=\quad \underbrace{p-5}_\text{sale price} \;+\; \underbrace{0.07(p-5)}_\text{tax on sale price} . \end{equation*}

    4.

    Write an expression for the sequence of operations.
    🔗
    (a)
    Subtract 9 from \(x\) and then multiply the result by 5.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(5(x-9)\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    Subtract \(x\) from 7 and then divide the result by 6.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\dfrac{7-x}{6} \text{,}\) or equivalently \(\frac{1}{6}(7-x)\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    Divide \(x\) by 3, subtract 4, and triple the result.
    🔗
    Solution.
    First we have \(\frac{x}{3}\text{,}\) and then subtracting \(4\) yields \(\frac{x}{3}-4\text{.}\) Finally, this is tripled (multiplied by \(3\)) to obtain \(3\pbr{\frac{x}{3}-4} \text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    Add 10 to \(x\text{,}\) then divide by 3, then square the result.
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(\pbr{\frac{x+10}{3} } ^2\)

    5.

    Given that \(f(x)=x^2+3x\text{,}\) \(g(x)=\sqrt{x^2-2}\text{,}\) and \(p(t)=10-3(t+1)\text{,}\) find each of the following.
    🔗
    (a)
    \(f(0)\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(f(0) = 0^2 + 3 \cdot 0 = 0\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    \(g(-4)\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(g(-4) = \sqrt{(-4)^2-2} = \sqrt{16-2} =\sqrt{14} \)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    \(p(-\frac{1}{2})\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(p\pbr{-\frac{1}{2}} = 10 - 3 \pbr{-\frac{1}{2}+1} = 10 - 3 \pbr{\frac{1}{2}} = 10-1.5 = 8.5\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    \(g(2t)\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(g(2t) = \sqrt{(2t)^2 - 2} = \sqrt{4t^2 - 2} \)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (e)
    \(p(x+h)\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \(p(x+h) = 10-3\pbr{(x+h)+1} = 10 - 3x-3h-3 = -3x-3h+7\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (f)
    \(f(3+h)-f(3)\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    We advise that you compute \(f(3+h)\) and \(f(3)\) separately. First,
    \begin{equation*} f(3+h) = (3+h)^2+3(3+h) = (3+h)(3+h) + 9 + 3h = 9+3h+3h+h^2+9+3h = h^2+9h+18. \end{equation*}
    Second,
    \begin{equation*} f(3) = 3^2 + 3(3) = 18. \end{equation*}
    Therefore
    \begin{equation*} f(3+h) - f(3) = h^2 + 9h + 18 - 18 = h^2 + 9h. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    6.

    Find the domain of each function.
    🔗
    (a)
    \(y=2x^2+3x-1\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    All real numbers.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    \(y=-3x+2\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    All real numbers.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    \(y=\sqrt{2x+5}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Because \(2x+5\) must be nonnegative, we require that \(2x+5\ge 0\text{.}\) Thus \(2x\ge -5\) and so \(x\ge -\frac{5}{2}\text{.}\) That is, the domain is all numbers greater than or equal to \(-\frac{5}{2}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    \(y=\dfrac{3}{7-2x}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Because \(7-2x\) cannot be zero (we cannot divide by zero), then \(7-2x\neq 0\) means that \(7\neq 2x\) and so \(x\neq \frac{7}{2}\text{.}\) That is, the domain is all numbers except for \(\frac{7}{2}\text{.}\)

    A driver of an SUV fills up his gas tank and starts a highway trip to a faraway city. Let \(G\text{,}\) in gallons, be the amount of gas left in the tank after driving \(d\) miles. Of course \(G\) is a function of \(d\text{;}\) that is, \(G=G(d)\text{.}\) Here is partial data about the function:
    🔗
    \(d\) \(0\) \(60\) \(120\) \(180\) \(240\) \(300\) \(360\) \(420\) \(480\) \(510\)
    \(G(d)\) \(17\) \(15\) \(13\) \(11\) \(9\) \(7\)
    Find gas mileage of the SUV; that is, the number of miles the SUV gets per gallon. Fill in the missing numbers.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Except for the last column when \(d=510\text{,}\) note that the miles \(d\) go up by \(60\) each time, and the gallons \(G(d)\) go down by \(2\) each time. Using these rules, the completed table is given below.
    🔗
    \(d\) \(0\) \(60\) \(120\) \(180\) \(240\) \(300\) \(360\) \(420\) \(480\) \(510\)
    \(G(d)\) \(17\) \(15\) \(13\) \(11\) \(9\) \(7\) \(5\) \(3\) \(1\) \(0\)
    Therefore, the car can drive \(510\) miles using \(17\) gallons of gas. Thus, the gas mileage is
    \begin{equation*} \frac{510 \text{ miles} }{17 \text{ gallons} } \;=\; 30 \frac{\text{miles} }{\text{gallon} } . \end{equation*}

    1.

    Find the horizontal and vertical intercepts of the following or state that they don’t exist.
    🔗
    (a)
    \(y=3x-1.5\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Setting \(y=0\text{,}\) then \(0=3x-1.5\) and so \(1.5=3x\) implying that \(x=1 /2\) is the horizontal intercept. The vertical intercept is when \(x=0\text{,}\) so \(y=-1.5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    \(f(x)=1-x^2\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Setting \(f(x)=0\text{,}\) then \(0=1-x^2\) and so \(x^2=1\text{.}\) Thus \(x=1\) and \(x=-1\) are the horizontal intercepts. The vertical intercept is when \(x=0\text{,}\) so \(y=1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    \(g(t)=5.4-0.2t\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Setting \(g(t)=0\text{,}\) then \(5.4-0.2t=0\) and so \(t=27\) is the horizontal intercept. When \(t=0\text{,}\) then \(y=5.4\) is the vertical intercept.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    \(y=2x^3+16\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Setting \(y=0\text{,}\) then \(2x^3+16=0\) and so \(2x^3=-16\text{.}\) Thus \(x^3=-8\) implying that \(x=\sqrt[3]{-8} \) is the horizontal intercept. When \(x=0\text{,}\) then \(y=16\) is the vertical intercept.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (e)
    \(h(m)=2\sqrt{m}-3\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Setting \(h(m)=0\text{,}\) then \(2\sqrt{m} -3=0\) so \(2\sqrt{m} =3\text{.}\) Thus \(\sqrt{m} = 3 /2\) implying that \(m = (3 /2)^2 = 9 /4\text{.}\) The vertical intercept is \(y=-3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (f)
    \(y=\dfrac{1}{x}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Note that \(y\) can never be \(0\text{,}\) so there are no horizontal intercepts. And, \(x\) cannot be \(0\) so there are no vertical intercepts either.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (g)
    \(y=\dfrac{1}{x-1}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Note that \(y\) can never be \(0\text{,}\) so there are no horizontal intercepts. But there is a vertical intercept when \(x=0\text{,}\) and this is \(y=-1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    A ball is dropped from a cliff above a lake. The ball’s height above the surface of the lake, \(h(t)\text{,}\) in feet, \(t\) seconds after the ball is dropped is given by
    \begin{equation*} h(t)=300-16t^2. \end{equation*}
    Find the vertical intercept of the function \(h(t)\) and the horizontal intercepts for which \(t\geq 0\text{.}\) (Round off to two decimal places.) Interpret the intercepts in practical terms.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The vertical intercept is \(y=h(0)=300\text{.}\) This occurs when \(t=0\text{,}\) so we can say that the ball was \(300\) feet above ground when it was dropped. The horizontal intercept is when \(y=h(t)=0\text{,}\) that is when \(0=300-16t^2\text{.}\) Solving for \(t\) we get
    \begin{equation*} 16t^2 = 300 \quad \implies \quad t^2 = \frac{300}{16} \quad \implies \quad t = \sqrt{300 / 16} \approx 4.33. \end{equation*}
    This means that \(4.33\) seconds after the ball was dropped, it hit the ground (because the height \(h(t)\) is \(0\)).

    The value of a car \(V(t)\text{,}\) in dollars, \(t\) years after the car was purchased is:
    \begin{equation*} V(t)=21500-1500t \end{equation*}
    Find vertical and horizontal intercepts of the function \(V(t)\) and interpret them in terms of dollars and years.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The vertical intercept is when \(t=0\text{,}\) which is \(V(0)=21500\text{.}\) This means that the car was worth \(\$21500\) at the time of purchase. The horizontal intercept is when \(V(t)=0\text{.}\) That is, \(21500-1500t=0\text{.}\) Solving this equation for \(t\) yields
    \begin{equation*} 21500 = 1500t \quad \implies \quad t = \frac{21500}{1500} \approx 14.33. \end{equation*}
    Therefore, \(14.33\) years after purchase the value of the car is \(\$0\text{.}\)

    Solution.

    Find the average rate of change of \(g(t)=3t^2+4\) between \(t=-2\) and \(t=1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    First, compute \(g(-2) = 3(-2)^2 + 4 = 16\) and \(g(1) = 3(1)^2+4=7\text{.}\) Then, the average rate of change is
    \begin{equation*} \frac{g(1)-g(-2)}{1-(-2)} = \frac{7-16}{1+2} = \frac{-9}{3} = -3. \end{equation*}
