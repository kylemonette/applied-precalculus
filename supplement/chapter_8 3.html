    1.

    A man takes \(3\) seconds to breathe in and out once. The volume of air in his lungs, \(V(t)\text{,}\) varies between a minimum of \(0.5\) liters and a maximum of \(4.5\) liters. Assume that at time \(t=0\) the volume is at a minimum; that is, time \(t=0\) is when the man begins to breathe in.
    🔗
    (a)
    What are the period, amplitude, and midline of \(V(t)\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The period is \(3\) seconds. The amplitude is
    \begin{equation*} \text{Amp. } \quad=\quad \frac{y_\text{max} -y_\text{min} }{2} \quad = \quad \frac{4.5-0.5}{2} \quad = \quad 2 \text{ liters}. \end{equation*}
    The midline equation is
    \begin{equation*} y \quad = \quad \frac{y_\text{max} + y_\text{min} }{2} \quad = \quad \frac{4.5+0.5}{2} \quad = \quad 2.5. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Estimate the period of the following periodic function \(f(t)\text{:}\)
    🔗
    \(t\) \(0\) \(1\) \(2\) \(3\) \(4\) \(5\) \(6\)
    \(f(t)\) \(12\) \(13\) \(14\) \(12\) \(13\) \(14\) \(12\)
    Solution.
    The outputs \(12\text{,}\) \(13\text{,}\) \(14\) are repeating in this pattern. And, it takes \(3\) units of \(x\) to do so, therefore the period of this function is \(3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Estimate the period of the following periodic function \(g(x)\text{:}\)
    🔗
    \(x\) \(1\) \(11\) \(21\) \(31\) \(41\) \(51\) \(61\) \(71\) \(81\)
    \(g(x)\) \(5\) \(3\) \(2\) \(3\) \(5\) \(3\) \(2\) \(3\) \(5\)
    Solution.
    The outputs \(5\text{,}\) \(3\text{,}\) \(2\text{,}\) \(3\) are repeating in this pattern. And, it takes \(40\) units of \(x\) to do so, therefore the period is \(40\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    For the graphs below, identify which could be periodic functions.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Graphs (A) and (B) are periodic meanwhile (C) and (D) are not.
    🔗
    In graph (C), the outputs are oscillating between the same numbers but the length of a cycle is constantly changing (always getting smaller).
    🔗
    In graph (D), the outputs are not oscillating between the same values. That is, the amplitude is appearing to grow as \(x\) increases.
