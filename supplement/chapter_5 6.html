    1.

    Write each exponential function in the form \(y=A\cdot b^t\text{.}\) Then identify the initial value and growth factor.
    🔗
    (a)
    \(y=3(6)^{2t}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    We can write \(y=3 (6^2)^t\) and so \(y=3(36)^t\text{,}\) meaning \(A=3\) and \(b=36\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    \(f(t)=6(0.5)^{t-1}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Using exponent rules, \(f(t)=6(0.5)^t (0.5)^{-1} = 6(0.5)^t \cdot 2 = 12 (0.5)^t\text{.}\) Therefore \(A=12\) and \(b=0.5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    \(g(t)=0.5\pbr{1-\dfrac{1.2}{6}}^{3t}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    We can simplify \(1-\frac{1.2}{6} = 0.8\) and so \(g(t)=0.5(0.8)^{3t}\text{.}\) Then, \(g(t)=0.5 (0.8^3)^t = 0.5(0.512)^t\) so \(A=0.5\) and \(b=0.512\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    \(y=\sqrt{9(0.01)^t}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Using exponent rules, \(y=\sqrt{9} \cdot \sqrt{(0.01)^t} = 3 (0.01^t)^{1 /2}\text{.}\) Then, \(y=3 \pbr{0.01^{1 /2}}^t = 3 (0.1)^t\) so \(A=3\) and \(b=0.1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (e)
    \(y=\dfrac{16(2)^t}{8(2)^{3t}}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Using exponent rules, \(y=\frac{16}{8} 2^{t-3t} = 2 \cdot 2^{-2t}\) and therefore \(y=2 \cdot (2^{-2})^t = 2 \cdot (0.25)^t\text{.}\) Therefore \(A=2\) and \(b=0.25\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (f)
    \(y=\pbr{(10)^2}^{-6t+1}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Simplifying \(10^2=100\) then \(y=100^{-6t+1}\) which is \(y=(100)^{-6t}\cdot 100 = (100^{-6})^t \cdot 100\text{.}\) Thus \(y=100 \cdot (100^{-6})^t\) and so \(A=100\) and \(b=100^{-6}\text{.}\)
    🔗

    2.

    Identify whether each exponential function represents exponential growth or exponential decay. Then identify the percent growth rate.
    🔗
    (a)
    \(y=1200(1.115)^t\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The growth factor is \(b=1.115\) which is greater than \(1\) so this describes exponential growth. The growth rate \(r\) is \(r=b-1=1.115-1 = 0.115 = 11.5\%\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    \(y=10(0.923)^x\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The growth factor is \(b=0.923 \lt 1\) so this describes exponential decay. The growth rate is \(r=b-1 = 0.923-1 = -0.077 = -7.7\%\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    \(f(x)=20(\sqrt{3})^x\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The growth factor is \(b=\sqrt{3} =1.73205 \gt 1\) so this describes exponential growth. The growth rate is \(r=b-1 = 1.73205-1 = 0.73205 = 73.205\%\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    \(P=50\pbr{\dfrac{3}{4}}^{-t}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    By noting that \(\pbr{\dfrac{3}{4}} ^{-t} = \pbr{\dfrac{4}{3}} ^t\text{,}\) then we have \(P = 50 \pbr{\frac{4}{3}} ^t\text{.}\) Thus the growth rate \(b=\frac{4}{3} \gt 1\) so this represents exponential growth. The growth rate is \(r=b-1 = \frac{4}{3}-1 = 0.333 = 33.3 \%\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (e)
    \(y=\dfrac{300}{5(3)^x}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    We can simplify \(y = \dfrac{300}{5} \cdot 3^{-x} = 60 \cdot \pbr{\dfrac{1}{3}} ^x\text{.}\) The growth factor is \(b=\frac{1}{3} \lt 1\) so this represents exponential decay. The growth rate is \(r=b-1 = \frac{1}{3}-1 = -\frac{2}{3} = -0.6666 = -66.6\%\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (f)
    \(Q=1000\pbr{1+\dfrac{0.05}{12}}^{12t}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    We can write \(Q = 1000 \pbr{1.00417} ^{12t}\) and so \(Q = 1000 \pbr{(1.00417)^{12}} ^t = 1000 \pbr{1.0512} ^t\text{.}\) Therefore the growth factor is \(b=1.0512 \gt 1\) so this describes exponential growth. The growth rate is \(r=b-1 = 1.0512-1 = 0.0512 = 5.12\%\text{.}\)

    Write an expression representing a population at time \(t\) years if it is initially 2 million and shrinking at a rate of \(3\%\) per year.
    🔗
    Solution.

    \begin{equation*} y = 2000000 (0.97)^t. \end{equation*}

    8.

    Write an expression representing the quantity of pollutant in a lake if the initial amount is \(Q_0\) and the amount of pollutant is growing at a rate of \(2\%\) a year for \(6\) years.
    🔗

    \begin{equation*} y = Q_0 (1.02)^t. \end{equation*}
    After \(t=6\) years, then the amount of pollutant in the lake would be
    \begin{equation*} y = Q_0 (1.02)^6. \end{equation*}

    🔗
    Solution.

    3.

    Find an equation for the exponential function \(f(t)=A\cdot b^t\) passing through the ordered pairs \((0,6)\) and \((4,16.2)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The first point \((0,6)\) means that the initial value is \(A = 6\text{.}\) Now, we know that \(f(t) = 6 \cdot b^t\) and we determine \(b\) now by plugging in the other point:
    \begin{equation*} 16.2 = 6 \cdot b^4 \quad \implies \quad 2.7 = b^4 \quad \implies \quad b = \sqrt[4]{2.7}=1.28186 . \end{equation*}
    Thus the function is
    \begin{equation*} f(t) = 6 (1.28186)^t. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4.

    Find an equation for the exponential function \(g(x)\) satisfying that \(g(6)=10\) and \(g(8)=5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Note that neither point is the initial value, so we need to create two equations and divide them. The equations are
    \begin{equation*} 10 = A \cdot b^6 \quad \text{and} \quad 5 = A \cdot b^8. \end{equation*}
    Dividing them, we get
    \begin{equation*} \frac{5}{10} = \frac{A\cdot b^8}{A\cdot b^6} \quad \implies \quad \frac{1}{2} = b^2 \quad \implies \quad b = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}} =0.70711. \end{equation*}
    Now, we can update the formula to
    \begin{equation*} g(x) = A \cdot (0.70711)^x \end{equation*}
    and solve for \(A\) by plugging in either of the given points. If we use the first one, say, then
    \begin{equation*} 10 = A (0.70711)^6 \quad \implies \quad A = 79.9978. \end{equation*}
    So the equation is then
    \begin{equation*} g(x) = 79.9978 \cdot (0.70711)^x. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗

    5.

    Find an equation for the exponential function \(h(t)\) satisfying that \(h(2)=1.2\) and \(h(4)=0.3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Note that neither point is the initial value, so we need to create two equations and divide them. The equations are
    \begin{equation*} 1.2 = A \cdot b^2 \quad \text{and} \quad 0.3 = A \cdot b^4. \end{equation*}
    Dividing them, we get
    \begin{equation*} \frac{0.3}{1.2} = \frac{A\cdot b^4}{A\cdot b^2} \quad \implies \quad b^2 = 0.25 \quad \implies \quad b = \sqrt{0.25} = 0.5. \end{equation*}
    Now, we can update the formula to
    \begin{equation*} h(t) = A \cdot (0.5)^t \end{equation*}
    and solve for \(A\) by plugging in either of the given points. If we use the first one, say, then
    \begin{equation*} 1.2 = A (0.5)^2 \quad \implies \quad A = 4.8. \end{equation*}
    So the function is then
    \begin{equation*} h(t) = 4.8\cdot (0.5)^t. \end{equation*}

    Exponential Functions.

    Could the table represent an exponential function \(y=A\cdot b^t\text{?}\) Why or why not? For those that could, find the formula.
    🔗
    1.
    \(t\) \(0\) \(1\) \(2\) \(3\)
    \(y\) \(81\) \(27\) \(9\) \(3\)
    Solution.
    The inputs are evenly spaced, and we multiply each output by \(\frac{1}{3}\) to go to the next one in the table. Therefore, this function is exponential. The initial value is \(81\text{,}\) so we have \(y=A b^t\text{.}\) Plugging in a point, say \((1,27)\text{,}\) tells us that
    \begin{equation*} 27=81 \cdot b^1 \end{equation*}
    so \(b=\frac{27}{81}=\frac{1}{3}\text{.}\) Therefore
    \begin{equation*} y=81\cdot (1 /3)^t. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    2.
    \(t\) \(0\) \(1\) \(2\) \(3\)
    \(y\) \(0\) \(2\) \(4\) \(8\)
    Solution.
    The inputs are evenly spaced, but we do not have a constant number that multiplies the outputs in the table. Indeed, the outputs are also evenly spaced (by \(2\)). Therefore this function is actually linear, and not exponential.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    3.
    \(t\) \(-1\) \(2\) \(5\) \(8\)
    \(y\) \(0.5\) \(2.5\) \(12.5\) \(62.5\)
    Solution.
    The inputs are evenly spaced (by \(3\)). There is a constant multiplier between outputs of \(5\text{.}\) To find this number, divide each pair of outputs: \(\frac{2.5}{0.5} =5\text{,}\) \(\frac{12.5}{2.5} =5\text{,}\) and \(\frac{62.5}{12.5} =5\). Therefore this function is exponential. The initial value is not provided, so we make two equations from two points and divide them. We have
    \begin{equation*} 2.5 = A \cdot b^2 \quad \text{and} \quad 0.5 = A \cdot b^{-1}. \end{equation*}
    Dividing these yields
    \begin{equation*} \frac{2.5}{0.5} = \frac{A\cdot b^2}{A\cdot b^{-1}} \quad \implies \quad 5 = b^3 \quad \implies \quad b = \sqrt[3]{5}=1.7099 . \end{equation*}
    Therefore \(y=A \cdot (1.7099)^t\) and we plug in a point, say \((-1,0.5)\text{,}\) to solve for \(A\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} 0.5 = A \cdot (1.7099)^{-1} \quad \implies \quad A = \frac{0.5}{(1.7099)^{-1}} = 0.8549. \end{equation*}
    So the equation is
    \begin{equation*} y = 0.8549 \cdot (1.7099)^t. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    4.
    \(t\) \(-4\) \(1\) \(6\) \(11\) \(16\)
    \(f(t)\) \(2.2\) \(1.8\) \(1.4\) \(1.0\) \(0.6\)
    Solution.
    The inputs are evenly spaced, however there is not a constant ratio between outputs. Rather, we subtract \(0.4\) from each output to go to the next. Therefore the function is actually linear and not exponential.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    5.
    \(t\) \(0\) \(3\) \(6\) \(9\)
    \(g(t)\) \(96\) \(24\) \(6\) \(1.5\)
    Solution.
    The inputs are evenly spaced, and there is a constant ratio of \(\frac{1}{4}\) between the outputs. To determine this, compute \(\frac{24}{96} =\frac{1}{4}\text{,}\) \(\frac{6}{24}=\frac{1}{4}\text{,}\) and \(\frac{1.5}{6} =\frac{1}{4}\text{.}\) Therefore the function is exponential. To find its formula, note that \(A=96\) was given. Then \(g(t)=96 \cdot b^t\) and we solve for \(b\) using another point, say \((3,24)\text{:}\)
    \begin{equation*} 24 = 96\cdot b^3 \quad \implies \quad b^3 = 0.25 \quad \implies \quad b = \sqrt[3]{0.25} = 0.62996 . \end{equation*}
    Therefore the formula is
    \begin{equation*} g(t) = 96 \cdot (0.62996)^t. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    6.
    \(x\) \(0\) \(3\) \(6\) \(9\) \(12\) \(15\)
    \(h(x)\) \(-2\) \(2\) \(-2\) \(-6\) \(-2\) \(2\)
    Solution.
    The inputs are evenly spaced, however at a first glance this cannot be exponential because we notice that the function is increasing and then decreasing. Exponential functions cannot do this; they always increase or always decrease. So this is not exponential.

    A trash dumpster starts with \(5\) pounds of garbage. Write a formula which represents the amount of garbage in the dumpster after \(t\) days given the following rates.
    🔗
    (a)
    The amount of garbage increases by \(3\) pounds per day.
    🔗
    Solution.
    This describes a linear relationship \(y=mt+b\) where the slope is \(m=3\) and the intercept is \(b=5\) so \(y=3t+5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    The amount of garbage increases by \(3\%\) per day.
    🔗
    Solution.
    This is an exponential relationship. The initial value is \(A=5\) and the growth rate is \(r=3\% = 0.03\) so the growth factor is \(b=1+r = 1.03\text{.}\) Therefore \(y=5(1.03)^t\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    The amount of garbage increases at a continuous rate of \(3\%\) per day.
    🔗
    Solution.
    This is an exponential relationship. The initial value is \(A=5\) and the continuous growth rate is \(k=3\% = 0.03\text{.}\) Therefore \(y=5 e^{0.03t}\text{.}\)

    3.

    A container with \(1\) liter of liquid is placed in a warm, arid environment. The liquid evaporates at a continuous rate of \(2.3\%\) per day.
    🔗
    (a)
    Write a function which represents the amount of liquid (in milliliters) in the container after \(t\) days.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The initial value is \(1\) liter so \(A=1000\) milliliters (be careful on the units!) and the continuous growth rate is \(k=-2.3\% = -0.023\text{.}\) Therefore \(y=1000 e^{-0.023t}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    What is the daily decay rate?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Recall that \(k=-0.023\) so \(b=e^{k} = e^{-0.023} = 0.97726\text{.}\) Thus the growth rate is \(r=b-1 = -0.02274 = -2.274\%\text{,}\) and then the decay rate is \(2.274\%\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    How much liquid remains after \(5\) days?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Using part (a), evaluate the function at \(t=5\) to get \(y=1000e^{-0.023(5)} = 891.366\) milliliters.

    4.

    Write a formula for each of the following.
    🔗
    (a)
    The amount of a radioactive substance present after \(t\) hours if there are initially \(500\) mg and the half-life is \(9\) hours.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Here we have \(A=500\) being the initial value and \(T=9\) hours being the half-life. Therefore
    \begin{equation*} y = 500 \pbr{\frac{1}{2}} ^{t /9} \end{equation*}
    See section 5.4 for this formula
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    The value of a car after \(t\) years if the initial price is \(\$26,\!000\) and the car depreciates at a rate of \(11\%\) per year.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The initial value \(A=26000\) and the growth rate is \(r=-11\% = -0.11\) so \(b=1+r = 1+(-0.11) = 0.89\text{.}\) Therefore
    \begin{equation*} y = 26000\cdot (0.89)^t. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    The value of a car after \(t\) years if the initial price is \(\$33,\!000\) and the car depreciates at a rate of \(\$3,\!700\) per year.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Because the value of the car decreases by a constant amount per year, this is a linear function (namely, not exponential). Therefore
    \begin{equation*} y = -3700t + 33000. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    The population of a city in year \(t\) (measured in years after 2005) if the population in 2005 is \(125,\!000\) and the city is growing at a continuous rate of \(3.1\%\) per year.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The initial value is \(125000\) and the continuous growth rate is \(k=3.1\%=0.031\text{.}\) Therefore
    \begin{equation*} y = 125000 e^{0.031t}. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (e)
    The number of ants in a colony if the initial colony size is \(6000\) ants and the number of ants increases by \(2.7\%\) each day.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The initial value is \(A = 6000\) and the growth rate \(r=2.7\%=0.027\) so \(b=1+r=1.027\text{.}\) Therefore
    \begin{equation*} y = 6000(1.027)^t. \end{equation*}
