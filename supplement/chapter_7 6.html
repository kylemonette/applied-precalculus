    Transforming Functions.

    Determine what function has been transformed and what transformations have been applied to it. Then graph the function.
    🔗
    1.
    \(y=x^2+10\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    For the original function \(f(x)=x^2\text{,}\) then \(y\) is \(f(x)\) shifted up by \(10\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    2.
    \(y=\sqrt[5]{x}-3\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    For the original function \(f(x)=\sqrt[5]{x} \text{,}\) then \(y\) is \(f(x)\) shifted down by \(3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    3.
    \(y=\ln(x+3)\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    For the original function \(f(x)=\ln (x)\text{,}\) then \(y\) is \(f(x)\) shifted left by \(3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    4.
    \(y=(x-5)^2\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    For the original function \(f(x)=x^2\text{,}\) then \(y\) is \(f(x)\) shifted right by \(5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    5.
    \(y=\dfrac{1}{x-7}+4\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    For the original function \(f(x)=\dfrac{1}{x}\text{,}\) then \(y\) is \(f(x)\) shifted right \(7\) and up \(4\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    6.
    \(y=e^{x-0.5}-7\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    For the original function \(f(x)=e^x\text{,}\) then \(y\) is \(f(x)\) shifted down \(7\) and right \(0.5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    7.
    \(y=(x+6)^2-2\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    For the original function \(f(x)=x^2\text{,}\) then \(y\) is \(f(x)\) shifted left \(6\) and down \(2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    8.
    \(y=2^{x+3}+8\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    For the original function \(f(x)=2^x\text{,}\) then \(y\) is \(f(x)\) shifted up \(8\) and left \(3\text{.}\)

    9.

    What transformations have been applied to the function \(f(x)=13x^2+2x-4\) to obtain \(g(x)=13(x-4)^2+2(x-4)+6\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Because the \(x\) in the function \(f(x)\) has been replaced with \(x-4\) in the function \(g(x)\text{,}\) this suggests that \(g(x)\) is the result of shifting \(f(x)\) to the right by \(4\text{.}\) And, the \(-4\) constant in \(f(x)\) is replaced with \(6\) in \(g(x)\text{,}\) which is \(10\) higher. Therefore \(g(x)\) is \(f(x)\) shifted up by \(10\text{.}\) In summary, \(g(x)\) is \(f(x)\) shifted right \(4\) and up by \(10\text{.}\) To confirm, \(g(x)=f(x-4)+10\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    10.

    Below is the graph of a function \(f(x)\text{.}\) Graph the function \(g(x)=f(x-4)-3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    To obtain \(g(x)\text{,}\) shift the graph of \(f(x)\) \(4\) units to the right and down \(3\) units. The graph in red is the graph of \(g(x)\text{.}\)

    11.

    Below is the graph of a transformation of the function \(y=x^2\text{.}\) Write a possible formula for it.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The red function on the graph is the plot of \(y=x^2\text{.}\) Therefore, the one in blue (i.e., the one we need a formula for) appears to have been shifted left by \(1\) and down by \(2\text{.}\) Therefore, this function is \(y=(x+1)^2-2\text{.}\)

    12.

    A function \(g(x)\) is formed by shifting the function \(f(x)\) left \(5\) and down \(6\text{.}\) Write a formula for \(g\) in terms of \(f\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Here, \(g(x)=f(x+5)-6\text{.}\)

    Here, \(g(x)=f(x-2.7)+14\text{.}\)

    Transforming Functions.

    Determine what function has been transformed and what transformations have been applied to it. Then graph the function.
    🔗
    1.
    \(y=5x^2\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    With the original function \(f(x)=x^2\text{,}\) then \(y\) is \(f(x)\) stretched vertically by a factor of \(5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    2.
    \(y=\dfrac{1}{7}\sqrt[3]{t}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    With the original function \(f(t)=\sqrt[3]{t} \text{,}\) then \(y\) is \(f(t)\) compressed vertically by a factor of \(7\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    3.
    \(y=\ln(6x)\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    With the original function \(f(x)=\ln (x)\text{,}\) then \(y\) is \(f(x)\) compressed horizontally by a factor of \(6\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    4.
    \(y=\pbr{\dfrac{1}{5}x}^2\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    With the original function \(f(x)=x^2\text{,}\) then \(y\) is \(f(x)\) stretched horizontally by a factor of \(5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    5.
    \(y=6\sqrt[5]{x}+8\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    With the original function \(f(x)=\sqrt[5]{x} \text{,}\) then \(y\) is \(f(x)\) stretched vertically by a factor of \(6\) and shifted up by \(8\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    6.
    \(y=-\dfrac{1}{4}(x-5)^2\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    With the original function \(f(x)=x^2\text{,}\) then \(y\) is \(f(x)\) compressed vertically by a factor of \(4\text{,}\) shifted right by \(5\text{,}\) and reflected over the \(x\)-axis.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    7.
    \(y=3e^{\frac{1}{2}x}+1\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    With the original function \(f(x)=e^x\text{,}\) then \(y\) is \(f(x)\) stretched horizontally by a factor of \(2\text{,}\) stretched vertically by a factor of \(3\text{,}\) and shifted up by \(1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    8.
    \(y=2^{3(x-0.5)}-5\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    With the original function \(f(x)=2^x\text{,}\) then \(y\) is \(f(x)\) shifted right by \(0.5\text{,}\) compressed horizontally by a factor of \(3\text{,}\) and shifted down \(5\) units.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    9.

    What transformations have been applied to the function \(f(x)=13x^2+2x-4\) to obtain \(g(x)=26(3x)^2+4(3x)-8\text{?}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Notice that we have the \((3x)\) terms in \(g(x)\text{,}\) suggesting that there is a horizontal scaling. And, the coefficients of \(26\text{,}\) \(4\text{,}\) and \(-8\) in \(g(x)\) are double those from \(f(x)\text{.}\) This suggests that we did a vertical scaling (i.e., multiply \(f(x)\) by \(2\) to get \(g(x)\)). In fact
    \begin{equation*} g(x) \quad = \quad 2 \pbr{13(3x)^2 + 2(3x) - 4} \quad = \quad 2 f(3x). \end{equation*}
    Therefore \(g\) is \(f\) compressed horizontally by a factor of \(3\) and stretched vertically by a factor of \(2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    11.

    Below is the graph of a transformation of the function \(y=x^2\) obtained by scaling. Write a possible formula for it.
    🔗
    Solution.
    In the graph below, we plot in red \(y=x^2\text{.}\) Notice that for \(y=x^2\) then we have the point \((1,1)\) but for the blue function we have \((2,1)\text{.}\) Similarly, \((2,4)\) on \(y=x^2\) corresponds with \((4,4)\) for the blue function.
    🔗
    That is, if the \(y\)-values are kept constant, then the \(x\)-values are being multiplied by \(2\text{.}\) Therefore, the function in blue is a horizontal scaling of \(y=x^2\text{,}\) namely a horizontal stretching by a factor of \(2\text{.}\) So, we have \(y=\pbr{\frac{1}{2}x} ^2\) for the formula.
    🔗
    We remark that you could interpret this as a vertical scaling instead of a horizontal one. Here, you would consider constant \(x\)-values and see what happens to the \(y\)-values. For example, the point \((2,4)\) on the \(y=x^2\) function aligns with \((2,1)\) on the blue function. Because \(1\) is one-fourth of \(4\text{,}\) then there is a vertical compression by a factor of \(4\text{,}\) and therefore \(y=\frac{1}{4}x^2\) is the formula. Notice that this is exactly the same formula as we got with the “horizontal scaling” approach, if you simplify that formula.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    12.

    A function \(g(x)\) is formed by reflecting the function \(f(x)\) over the \(y\)-axis, stretching it vertically by a factor of \(5\text{,}\) and shifting it down \(6\text{.}\) Write a formula for \(g\) in terms of \(f\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Here we have \(g(x)=5f(-x)-6\text{.}\)

    13.

    A function \(g(x)\) is formed by shifting the function \(f(x)\) right \(0.6\text{,}\) stretching it horizontally by a factor of \(3\text{,}\) compressing it vertically by a factor of \(2\text{,}\) and shifting it up \(14\text{.}\) Write a formula for \(g\) in terms of \(f\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Here we have
    \begin{equation*} g(x) = \frac{1}{2} f\pbr{\frac{1}{3}(x-0.6)} +14. \end{equation*}

    Worksheet 7.3 Practice Worksheet
    

    1.

    Let \(f(x)=2x-1\text{,}\) \(g(x)=3x\text{,}\) and \(h(x)=x^2+1\text{.}\) Find each of the following.
    🔗
    (a)
    \(f(g(x))\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} f(g(x)) \quad = \quad f(3x) \quad = \quad 2(3x)-1 \quad = \quad 6x-1. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    \(h(g(x))\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} h(g(x)) \quad = \quad h(3x) \quad = \quad (3x)^2+1 \quad = \quad 9x^2+1. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    \(g(f(x))\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} g(f(x)) \quad = \quad g(2x-1) \quad = \quad 3(2x-1) \quad= \quad 6x-3. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    \(f(g(-3))\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Option 1: Use the formula in part (a), plugging in \(x=-3\text{.}\) Then \(f(g(-3)) = 6(-3)-1 = -19\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Option 2: Evaluate the composition directly. Since \(g(-3)=-9\text{,}\) then \(f(g(-3)) = f(-9) = -19\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (e)
    \(f(h(7))\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Option 1: Find the formula for \(f(h(x))\) if you want!
    🔗
    Option 2: Evaluate the composition directly. Since \(h(7) = 50\text{,}\) then \(f(h(7)) = f(50) = 2(50)-1=99\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (f)
    \(f(g(h(2)))\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Option 1: Find the formula for \(f(g(h(x)))\) if you want, but this is a LOT of work!
    🔗
    Option 2: (Recommended!) Evaluate the composition directly, working inside-out. Note that \(h(2)=2^2+1=5\text{.}\) Then, \(g(h(2)) = g(5) = 3(5)=15\text{.}\) Lastly, \(f(g(h(2))) = f(15) = 2(15)-1=29\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    Let \(f(x)=-3x+7\) and \(g(x)=2x^2-8\text{.}\) Find the following.
    🔗
    (a)
    \(f(g(x))\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{align*} f(g(x)) \amp \quad =\quad f(2x^2-8) \\ \amp \quad = \quad -3(2x^2-8)+7 \\ \amp \quad= \quad -6x^2+24+7 \\ \amp \quad = \quad -6x^2+31. \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    \(g(f(x))\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{equation*} g(f(x)) \quad = \quad g(-3x+7) \quad = \quad 2(-3x+7)^2-8. \end{equation*}
    If you wish, you may find it advantageous to practice expanding this out:
    🔗
    \begin{align*} 2(-3x+7)^2-8 \amp \quad = \quad 2(-3x+7)(-3x+7)-8\\ \amp \quad = \quad 2(9x^2-21x-21x+49)-8\\ \amp \quad = \quad 18x^2-84x+90 \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    Identify functions \(f(x)\) and \(g(x)\) to rewrite each of the following as a composition of the form \(f(g(x))\text{.}\)
    🔗
    (a)
    \(y=(4x+3)^2\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    A good rule of thumb is that we want to choose the inner function — in this case \(g(x)\) — to be the “function you would put a number into first” if you were evaluating the original function. So, here we could pick
    \begin{equation*} g(x) = 4x+3, \qquad f(x)=x^2. \end{equation*}
    To check this, note that
    \begin{equation*} f(g(x)) \quad = \quad f(4x+3) \quad = \quad (4x+3)^2 \end{equation*}
    as desired.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    \(y=(x-1)^2+2(x-1)-8\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Here we can pick
    \begin{equation*} g(x) = x-1, \qquad f(x)=x^2+2x-8. \end{equation*}
    Verify on your own that this works by computing \(f(g(x))\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    \(y=\sqrt{x^2+7}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Here we can pick
    \begin{equation*} g(x) = x^2+7, \qquad f(x)=\sqrt{x} . \end{equation*}
    Another correct choice is to pick
    \begin{equation*} g(x) = x^2, \qquad f(x) = \sqrt{x+7} . \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    \(y=\dfrac{1}{3x^7+2}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Here we can pick
    \begin{equation*} g(x) = 3x^7 + 2, \qquad f(x)=\frac{1}{x}. \end{equation*}
    Another correct choice is to pick
    \begin{equation*} g(x) = 3x^7, \qquad f(x) = \frac{1}{x+2}. \end{equation*}
    Yet another is to use
    \begin{equation*} g(x) = x^7, \qquad f(x)=\frac{1}{3x+2}. \end{equation*}
