    Worksheet 3.1 Practice Worksheet
    

    1.

    Write each quadratic function in the standard form \(ax^2+bx+c\) and identify \(a\text{,}\) \(b\text{,}\) and \(c\text{.}\) For each function, what is the \(y\)-intercept? Will its graph open up or down?
    🔗
    (a)
    \(y=(-4x)^2+1\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Because \((-4x)^2 = 16x^2\text{,}\) then the equation in standard form is
    \begin{equation*} y = 16x^2 + 1. \end{equation*}
    This means \(a=16\text{,}\) \(b=0\text{,}\) and \(c=1\text{.}\) Because \(a\) is positive, the quadratic opens up. And the vertical intercept is \(1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (b)
    \(f(x)=(x-6)^2-20\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    First, expand \((x-6)^2\) by multiplying \((x-6)(x-6)\) to get
    \begin{equation*} f(x) = (x-6)(x-6)-20 = (x^2-6x-6x+36)-20 = x^2-12x+16. \end{equation*}
    This means \(a=1\text{,}\) \(b=-12\text{,}\) and \(c=16\text{.}\) Because \(a\) is positive, the graph opens up. And the vertical intercept is \(16\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (c)
    \(g(x)=2(x-3)^2-16\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Expand the \((x-3)^2\) term by computing \((x-3)(x-3)\text{:}\)
    🔗
    \begin{align*} g(x) \amp= 2(x-3)(x-3)-16\\ \amp= 2 \pbr{x^2-3x-3x+9} -16\\ \amp = 2\pbr{x^2-6x+9} -16\\ \amp= 2x^2-12x+18-16\\ \amp= 2x^2-12x + 2 \end{align*}
    🔗
    So \(a=2\text{,}\) \(b=-12\text{,}\) and \(c=2\text{.}\) This means the graph opens up and has a vertical intercept of \(2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (d)
    \(y=-(x+1)(x-5)\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Expand the product to get
    \begin{equation*} y = - \pbr{x^2-5x+x-5} = - \pbr{x^2-4x-5} = -x^2+4x+5. \end{equation*}
    Be careful of the negative sign in front! Now, \(a=-1\text{,}\) \(b=4\text{,}\) and \(c=5\) so the graph opens down (because \(a\) is negative) and has a vertical intercept of \(5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    (e)
    \(h(x)=(x-2)(x+7)-3(2x-5)\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    \begin{align*} h(x) \amp= x^2+7x-2x-14 - 3(2x-5)\\ \amp= x^2+5x-14 - 6x + 15\\ \amp= x^2-x+1 \end{align*}
    🔗
    Therefore \(a=1\text{,}\) \(b=-1\text{,}\) and \(c=1\text{,}\) so the graph opens up and has a vertical intercept at \(1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2.

    A quadratic parabola crosses the \(x\)-axis at the point \((2,0)\) and has a vertex of \((4,4)\text{.}\) Find the second point on the \(x\)-axis where the parabola crosses the \(x\)-axis. Does the parabola open up or open down?
    🔗
    Solution.
    Recall that \(h=\dfrac{x_1+x_2}{2} \text{.}\) We know \(x_1=2\) and \(h=4\) (the \(x\)-coordinate of the vertex). Therefore
    \begin{equation*} 4=\frac{2+x_2}{2} . \end{equation*}
    Solving for \(x_2\text{,}\) we multiply both sides by \(2\) to get \(8 = 2+x_2\) and so \(x_2=6\text{.}\) This means that the function has another \(x\)-intercept at \(x=6\text{.}\) To determine if it opens up or down, note that the vertex is above the \(x\)-axis (because \(k=4\text{,}\) the \(y\)-coordinate of the vertex). So the graph must open down, because we know it has two horizontal intercepts and the vertex is above the \(x\)-axis.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3.

    The horizontal intercepts of a quadratic parabola are \(x=-2\) and \(x=5\text{.}\) Find the \(x\)-coordinate of its vertex.
    🔗
    Solution.
    The \(x\)-coordinate of the vertex is \(h\text{,}\) and so we use
    \begin{equation*} h = \frac{x_1+x_2}{2} . \end{equation*}
    Because \(x_1=-2\) and \(x_2=5\text{,}\) then
    \begin{equation*} h = \frac{-2+5}{2} = \frac{3}{2}. \end{equation*}

    Finding Intercepts.

    Find the horizontal intercepts of each quadratic function by factoring.
    🔗
    1.
    \(f(x)=x^2+3 x+2\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Two numbers that multiply to \(2\) and add to \(3\) are \(1\) and \(2\text{,}\) so \(f(x)=(x+2)(x+1)\text{.}\) Setting this to \(0\text{,}\) then \(x+2=0\) and \(x+1=0\text{,}\) meaning \(x=-2\) and \(x=-1\) are the horizontal intercepts.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    2.
    \(y=x^2-x-6\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Two numbers that multiply to \(-6\) and add to \(-1\) are \(-3\) and \(2\text{,}\) so \(y=(x-3)(x+2)\text{.}\) Setting this to \(0\text{,}\) then \(x-3=0\) and \(x+2=0\text{,}\) meaning \(x=3\) and \(x=-2\) are the horizontal intercepts.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    3.
    \(g(t)=t^2+t-6\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Two numbers that multiply to \(-6\) and add to \(1\) are \(3\) and \(-2\text{,}\) so \(g(t)=(t+3)(t-2)\text{.}\) Setting this to \(0\text{,}\) then \(t+3=0\) and \(t-2=0\text{,}\) meaning \(t=-3\) and \(t=2\) are the horizontal intercepts.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    4.
    \(y=x^2-8 x+16\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Two numbers that multiply to \(16\) and add to \(-8\) are \(-4\) and \(-4\text{,}\) so \(y=(x-4)(x-4)\text{.}\) Setting this to \(0\text{,}\) then \(x-4=0\) and so \(x=4\) is the only horizontal intercept.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    5.
    \(f(w)=w^2+14 w+49\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Two numbers that multiply to \(49\) and add to \(14\) are \(7\) and \(7\text{,}\) so \(f(w)=(w+7)(w+7)\text{.}\) Setting this to \(0\text{,}\) then \(w+7=0\) and so \(w=-7\) is the only horizontal intercept.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    6.
    \(f(t)=64t^2-36\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    One method (the preferred method) is to realize this is a difference of squares. That is, \(f(t)=(8t)^2-6^2\) and so \(f(t)=(8t+6)(8t-6)\text{.}\) In this case, setting \(f(t)=0\) yields \(8t-6=0\) so \(t=\frac{6}{8}=\frac{3}{4}\) and also \(8t+6=0\) so \(t=-\frac{3}{4}\text{.}\) Another method is to factor out \(64\) and write \(f(t)=64(t^2-9 / 16)\text{.}\) Then, we need two numbers that multiply to \(-9/16\) and add to \(0\text{,}\) which are \(-3/4\) and \(3 /4\text{.}\) Thus \(f(t)=64(t-3/4)(t+3/4)\) which is actually equal to the other method above (as we expect) if you distribute the \(64\) inside.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    7.
    \(h(x)=2 x^2+10 x+8\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Note that \(2\) is a factor in each term, so we can write \(h(x) = 2 (x^2+5x+4)\text{.}\) Now, concentrate on \(x^2+5x+4\text{.}\) Two numbers that multiply to \(4\) and add to \(5\) are \(4\) and \(1\text{,}\) so this factors to \((x+4)(x+1)\text{.}\) Therefore \(h(x)=2(x+4)(x+1)\text{.}\) Horizontal intercepts are \(x=-4\) and \(x=-1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    8.
    \(y=2 w^2- w-3\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Unlike the last question, \(2\) is not a factor in every term. So we use the \(ac\)-method (described in the textbook). First, find two numbers which multiply to \(a\cdot c = 2 \cdot (-3)=-6\) and add to \(-1\text{,}\) which are \(2\) and \(-3\text{.}\) Then, write \(y = 2w^2 +2w - 3w - 3\text{.}\) Factoring out the greatest common factors,
    \begin{equation*} y = 2w(w+1) - 3\cdot (w+1) \end{equation*}
    which factors to \(y=(w+1)(2w-3)\text{.}\) Setting this to \(0\text{,}\) then \(w+1=0\) and \(2w-3=0\text{.}\) Thus horizontal intercepts are \(w=-1\) and \(w=\frac{3}{2}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    9.
    \(f(x)=-x^2-6x+16\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Factor out the \(-1\) from everything, yielding \(f(x)=-(x^2+6x-16)\text{.}\) Then, concentrate on \(x^2+6x-16\text{.}\) Two numbers which multiply to \(-16\) and add to \(6\) are \(8\) and \(-2\text{.}\) Therefore \(f(x)=-(x+8)(x-2)\text{.}\) Setting this to \(0\text{,}\) then \(x+8=0\) and \(x-2=0\text{,}\) thus \(x=-8\) and \(x=2\) are the horizontal intercepts.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    10.
    \(g(x)=\frac{1}{49}-4x^2\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    This is another difference of squares since \(g(x)=\pbr{\frac{1}{7}} ^2 - (2x)^2\text{.}\) Therefore \(g(x)=\pbr{\frac{1}{7}-2x} \pbr{\frac{1}{7}+2x} \text{.}\) This means that the horizontal intercepts are when \(\frac{1}{7}-2x=0\) and \(\frac{1}{7}+2x=0\text{,}\) that is, when \(x=\frac{1}{14}\) and \(x=-\frac{1}{14}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    11.
    \(p(x)=2x^2-6x\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The factor \(2x\) is common to both terms, so \(p(x)=2x(x-3)\) and therefore the horizontal intercepts are when \(x=0\) and \(x=3\text{.}\) Alternatively, you can just factor out the \(2\) and then write \(p(x)=2(x^2-3x)\text{.}\) From here, find two numbers that multiply to \(0\) and add to \(-3\text{.}\) This will yield the same answer as the above.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    12.
    \(m(t)=-5t^2+2t\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    The greatest common factor in each term is \(t\text{,}\) so \(m(t)=t(-5t+2)\text{.}\) Then, horizontal intercepts are when \(t=0\) and when \(-5t+2=0\text{,}\) that is, \(t=0\) and \(t=\frac{2}{5}\text{.}\)

    Standard Form to Vertex Form.

    Complete the square to convert each quadratic function from standard form to vertex form. Then identify the vertex.
    🔗
    1.
    \(y=x^2+8x\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Here, \(b=8\) so \(\frac{b}{2}=4\) and \(\pbr{\frac{b}{2}} ^2 = 16\text{.}\) Adding and subtracting this, we get
    \begin{equation*} y = x^2+8x+16-16 \end{equation*}
    which factors to
    \begin{equation*} y = (x+4)^2-16. \end{equation*}
    The vertex is \((-4,-16)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    2.
    \(y=4x^2-12x\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    First factor out the \(4\) to get \(y=4(x^2-3x)\text{.}\) Now concentrate only on the \(x^2-3x\) quadratic. Here, \(b=-3\) so \(\frac{b}{2}=-\frac{3}{2}\) and \(\pbr{\frac{b}{2}} ^2 = \frac{9}{4}\text{.}\) Thus
    \begin{equation*} x^2-3x \quad=\quad x^2-3x + \frac{9}{4} - \frac{9}{4} \quad=\quad \pbr{x-\frac{3}{2}} ^2-\frac{9}{4}. \end{equation*}
    Multiplying back by the \(4\) we factored out originally,
    \begin{equation*} y \quad=\quad 4\pbr{\pbr{x-\frac{3}{2}} ^2 - \frac{9}{4}} \quad=\quad 4\pbr{x-\frac{3}{2}} ^2-9. \end{equation*}
    The vertex is \(\pbr{\frac{3}{2}, -9} \text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    3.
    \(y=x^2-2x-15\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Here \(b=-2\) so \(\frac{b}{2}=-1\) and \(\pbr{\frac{b}{2}} ^2=1\text{.}\) Adding and subtracting this,
    \begin{align*} y \amp \quad=\quad x^2-2x+1-15-1 \\ \amp \quad=\quad (x-1)^2-15-1\\ \amp \quad=\quad (x-1)^2-16. \end{align*}
    The vertex is \((1,-16)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    4.
    \(y=3x^2-20x+7\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    First factor out the \(3\) from the first two terms to get \(y=3(x^2-\frac{20}{3}x) + 7\text{.}\) Now, concentrate only on the \(x^2-\frac{20}{3}x\) quadratic. Here, \(b=-\frac{20}{3}\) so \(\frac{b}{2}=-\frac{10}{3}\) and \((\frac{b}{2})^2 = \frac{100}{9}\text{.}\) Adding and subtracting this,
    \begin{align*} x^2-\frac{20}{3}x \amp \quad=\quad x^2 - \frac{20}{3}x + \frac{100}{9} - \frac{100}{9} \\ \amp \quad=\quad \pbr{x-\frac{10}{3}} ^2 - \frac{100}{9}. \end{align*}
    Now, multiply by the \(3\) that we factored out initially to get
    \begin{align*} y \amp \quad=\quad 3 \pbr{\pbr{x-\frac{10}{3}} ^2 - \frac{100}{9}} + 7 \\ \amp \quad=\quad 3\pbr{x-\frac{10}{3}} ^2 - \frac{100}{3} + 7 \\ \amp \quad=\quad 3\pbr{x-\frac{10}{3}} ^2 - \frac{79}{3}. \end{align*}
    The vertex is \((\frac{10}{3}, -\frac{79}{3})\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    5.
    \(y=5x^2-10x+48\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Factor out the \(5\) from the first two terms to get \(y=5(x^2-2x)+48\text{.}\) Now, concentrate only on the \(x^2-2x\) quadratic. Here, \(b=-2\) and so \(\frac{b}{2}=-1\) and \(\pbr{\frac{b}{2}} ^2=1\text{.}\) Therefore
    \begin{equation*} x^2-2x \quad = \quad x^2-2x+1-1 \quad = \quad (x-1)^2-1. \end{equation*}
    Now, multiply by the \(5\) that we factored out initially to get
    \begin{align*} y \amp \quad = \quad 5\pbr{(x-1)^2-1} + 48 \\ \amp \quad = \quad 5(x-1)^2-5+48 \\ \amp \quad = \quad 5(x-1)^2+43. \end{align*}
    The vertex is \((1,43)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    6.
    \(y=4x^2-2x+1\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Factor out the \(4\) from the first two terms to get \(y=4(x^2-\frac{1}{2}x) + 1\text{.}\) Now, concentrate only on the \(x^2-\frac{1}{2}x\) quadratic. Here, \(b=-\frac{1}{2}\) so \(\frac{b}{2}=-\frac{1}{4}\) and \(\pbr{\frac{b}{2}} ^2 = \frac{1}{16}\text{.}\) Therefore
    \begin{align*} x^2-\frac{1}{2}x \amp \quad = \quad x^2 - \frac{1}{2}x + \frac{1}{16} - \frac{1}{16}\\ \amp \quad = \quad \pbr{x-\frac{1}{4}}^2 - \frac{1}{16}. \end{align*}
    Now, multiply by the \(4\) that we factored out initially to get
    \begin{align*} y \amp \quad = \quad 4 \pbr{\pbr{x-\frac{1}{4}} ^2-\frac{1}{16}} + 1 \\ \amp \quad = \quad 4\pbr{x-\frac{1}{4}} ^2 - \frac{4}{16} + 1 \\ \amp \quad = \quad 4\pbr{x-\frac{1}{4}} ^2 +\frac{3}{4}. \end{align*}
    The vertex is \(\pbr{\frac{1}{4}, \frac{3}{4}} \text{.}\)

    Solving Quadratic Equations.

    Use an appropriate method to solve each quadratic equation.
    🔗
    1.
    \((x+1)(x-5)=0\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Because this is already factored, set each factor to \(0\text{.}\) Therefore \(x+1=0\) and \(x-5=0\text{,}\) implying \(x=-1\) and \(x=5\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    2.
    \((4k+5)(k-1)=0\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Because this is already factored, set each factor to \(0\text{.}\) Therefore \(4k+5=0\) and \(k-1=0\text{,}\) implying \(k=-\frac{5}{4}\) and \(k=1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    3.
    \(x^2-13x-14=0\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    This can be factored to \((x-14)(x+1)=0\text{.}\) Therefore, \(x=14\) and \(x=-1\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    4.
    \(4n^2-25=0\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    This is a difference of squares: \((2n)^2-5^2=0\text{.}\) Therefore it can be factored to \((2n+5)(2n-5)=0\text{.}\) Of course, you could use the \(ac\)-method in section 3.2 as well. Now, \(2n+5=0\) so \(n=-\frac{5}{2}\) and \(2n-5=0\) so \(n=\frac{5}{2}\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    5.
    \(3x^2+5x-2=0\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Here we’ll use the quadratic formula:
    🔗
    \begin{align*} x \amp \quad = \quad \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2-4ac} }{2a}\\ \amp \quad = \quad \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{5^2-4(3)(-2)} }{2(3)}\\ \amp \quad = \quad \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{25+24} }{6}\\ \amp \quad = \quad \frac{-5 \pm \sqrt{49} }{6}\\ \amp \quad = \quad \frac{-5 \pm 7}{6} \end{align*}
    🔗
    Now, \(\frac{-5+7}{6} = \frac{2}{6}=\frac{1}{3}\) and \(\frac{-5-7}{6} = \frac{-12}{6}=-2\) so the solutions are \(x=\frac{1}{3}\) and \(x=-2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    6.
    \(x^2+8x=-15\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    First, rewrite the equation as \(x^2+8x+15=0\text{.}\) This factors to \((x+5)(x+3)=0\text{.}\) Therefore, \(x+5=0\) and \(x+3=0\text{,}\) implying the solutions are \(x=-5\) and \(x=-3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    7.
    \(x(x-20)=-100\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    We must first distribute the \(x\) to get \(x^2 - 20x = -100\) and then add \(100\) to both sides:
    \begin{equation*} x^2-20x+100=0. \end{equation*}
    Now, we can factor this equation to \((x-10)(x-10)=0\text{,}\) implying the solution is \(x=10\text{.}\) If you did not do this first step, you might be saying that from \(x(x-20)=-100\) it follows that \(x=-100\) and \(x-20=-100\text{.}\) This is NOT true! Remember the zero-product rule only works if the equation is set to \(0\text{,}\) and not some other number like \(-100\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    8.
    \(9y^2-11=6y\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Subtract \(6y\) from both sides to obtain \(9y^2-6y-11=0\text{.}\) Using the quadratic formula, then
    🔗
    \begin{align*} y \amp \quad = \quad \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2-4ac} }{2a}\\ \amp \quad = \quad \frac{6 \pm \sqrt{(-6)^2-4(9)(-11)} }{2(9)}\\ \amp \quad = \quad \frac{6\pm \sqrt{36+396} }{18}\\ \amp \quad = \quad \frac{6 \pm \sqrt{432} }{18} \qquad \text{(can leave it like this)} \end{align*}
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Computing the Discriminant.

    Compute the discriminant of each quadratic equation. What does it tell you about the number of solutions that the equation will have?
    🔗
    9.
    \(6p^2-2p-3=0\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Here \(a=6\text{,}\) \(b=-2\text{,}\) and \(c=-3\) so the discriminant is
    \begin{equation*} D \quad = \quad b^2-4ac \quad = \quad (-2)^2-4(6)(-3) \quad = \quad 76. \end{equation*}
    Because \(D \gt 0\text{,}\) there are two distinct real solutions.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    10.
    \(9n^2-3n+2=0\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Here \(a=9\text{,}\) \(b=-3\text{,}\) and \(c=2\) so the discriminant is
    \begin{equation*} D \quad = \quad b^2-4ac \quad = \quad (-3)^2-4(9)(2) \quad = \quad -63. \end{equation*}
    Because \(D \lt 0\text{,}\) there are no real solutions.
    🔗

    11.
    \(x^2-2x-15=0\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Here \(a=1\text{,}\) \(b=-2\text{,}\) and \(c=-15\) so the discriminant is
    \begin{equation*} D \quad = \quad b^2-4ac \quad = \quad (-2)^2-4(1)(-15) \quad = \quad 64. \end{equation*}
    Because \(D \gt 0\text{,}\) there are two distinct real solutions.

    15.

    Find the formula for the quadratic function whose graph has \(x\)-intercepts \((4,0)\) and \((10,0)\) and \(y\)-intercept \((0,20)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Given the \(x\)-intercepts \(x_1=4\) and \(x_2=10\text{,}\) then using factored form we have
    \begin{equation*} y \quad = \quad a(x-x_1)(x-x_2) \quad = \quad a(x-4)(x-10). \end{equation*}
    We need the other point \((0,20)\) to determine \(a\text{.}\) Plugging this in,
    \begin{equation*} 20 \quad = \quad a(0-4)(0-10). \end{equation*}
    Therefore, \(20=a(-4)(-10)\) so \(20=a(40)\) and thus \(a=\frac{1}{2}\text{.}\) So
    \begin{equation*} y = \frac{1}{2}(x-4)(x-10). \end{equation*}

    16.

    Find the formula for the quadratic function whose graph has vertex \((-3,8)\) and \(y\)-intercept \((0,-5)\text{.}\)
    🔗
    Solution.
    Given the vertex \(h=-3\) and \(k=8\text{,}\) then
    \begin{equation*} y \quad = \quad a(x-h)^2+k \quad = \quad a(x+3)^2+8. \end{equation*}
    We use the other point \((0,-5)\) to solve for \(a\text{.}\) Plugging this in above,
    \begin{equation*} -5 \quad = \quad a(0+3)^2+8. \end{equation*}
    This means that \(-5 = a(9)+8\) so \(-13=9a\) implying that \(a=\frac{-13}{9} \text{.}\) So
    \begin{equation*} y = -\frac{13}{9} (x+3)^2+8. \end{equation*}

    17.

    Find the equation in factored form for the graphed quadratic function.
    🔗
    Solution.
    One approach is to use the two horizontal intercepts \(x_1=-5\) and \(x_2=3\text{.}\) Then
    \begin{equation*} y \quad = \quad a(x-x_1)(x-x_2) \quad = \quad a(x+5)(x-3). \end{equation*}
    To determine \(a\text{,}\) we need to use another point on the graph, say \((-1,12)\text{.}\) Plugging this in,
    \begin{equation*} 12 \quad = \quad a(-1+5)(-1-3) \quad = \quad a(4)(-4). \end{equation*}
    Thus \(12=-16a\) and so \(a=\frac{-12}{16} = \frac{-3}{4} \text{.}\) This means
    \begin{equation*} y = \frac{-3}{4} (x+5)(x-3). \end{equation*}
    Note that using the vertex \((-1,12)\text{,}\) like the next question, will give you an equation for the quadratic function but it will not be in factored form as this problem is requiring.

    18.

    Find the equation in vertex form for the graphed quadratic function.
    🔗
    Solution.
    We use the vertex \((2,-12)\) here to get
    \begin{equation*} y \quad = \quad a(x-2)^2-12. \end{equation*}
    To determine \(a\text{,}\) use another point on the graph like \((6,0)\) for example. Plugging this in,
    \begin{equation*} 0 \quad = \quad a(6-2)^2-12. \end{equation*}
    Therefore \(0=16a-12\) and so \(a=\frac{3}{4}\text{.}\) This implies
    \begin{equation*} y = \frac{3}{4}(x-2)^2-12. \end{equation*}
    Note that using the two horizontal intercepts, like the previous question, will give you an equation for the quadratic function but it will not be in vertex form as this problem is requiring.
