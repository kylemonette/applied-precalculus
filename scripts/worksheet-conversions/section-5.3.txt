\begin{questions}
\question Could the table represent an exponential function $y=A\cdot b^t$? Why or why not? For those that could, find the formula.

\begin{parts}

\part
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline
$t$ & $0$ & $1$ & $2$ & $3$ \\\hline
$y$ & $81$ & $27$ & $9$ & $3$ \\\hline
\end{tabular}

\begin{solution}[9em]
The inputs are evenly spaced, and we multiply each output by $\frac{1}{3}$ to go to the next one in the table. Therefore, this function is exponential.

The initial value is $81$, so we have $y=A b^t$. Plugging in a point, say $(1,27)$, tells us that
\[
27=81 \cdot b^1
\] so $b=\frac{27}{81}=\frac{1}{3}$. Therefore
\[
y=81\cdot (1 /3)^t.
\]
\end{solution}


\part
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline 
$t$ & $0$ & $1$ & $2$ & $3$ \\\hline
$y$ & $0$ & $2$ & $4$ & $8$ \\\hline
\end{tabular}

\begin{solution}[7em]
The inputs are evenly spaced, but we do not have a constant number that multiplies the outputs in the table. Indeed, the outputs are also evenly spaced (by $2$). Therefore this function is actually {\it linear}, and not exponential.
\end{solution}


\part
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline
$t$ &$ -1$ & $2$ & $5$ & $8$ \\\hline
$y$ & $0.5$ & $2.5$ & $12.5$ & $62.5$ \\\hline
\end{tabular}

\begin{solution}[9em]
The inputs are evenly spaced (by $3$). There is a constant multiplier between outputs of $5$. {\it To find this number, divide each pair of outputs: $\frac{2.5}{0.5} =5$, $\frac{12.5}{2.5} =5$, and $\frac{62.5}{12.5} =5$}. Therefore this function is exponential.

The initial value is not provided, so we make two equations from two points and divide them. We have
\[
2.5 = A \cdot b^2 \quad \text{and} \quad 0.5 = A \cdot b^{-1}
.\] Dividing these yields
\[
\frac{2.5}{0.5}  = \frac{A\cdot b^2}{A\cdot b^{-1}} \quad \implies \quad 5 = b^3 \quad \implies \quad b = \sqrt[3]{5}=1.7099 
.\] Therefore $y=A \cdot (1.7099)^t$ and we plug in a point, say $(-1,0.5)$, to solve for $A$:
\[
0.5 = A \cdot (1.7099)^{-1} \quad \implies \quad A = \frac{0.5}{(1.7099)^{-1}}  = 0.8549
.\] So the equation is
\[
y = 0.8549 \cdot (1.7099)^t
.\] 
\end{solution}


\part
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|} \hline
$t$ & $-4$ & $1$ & $6$ & $11$ & $16$ \\\hline 
$f(t)$ & $2.2$ & $1.8$ & $1.4$ & $1.0$ & $0.6$ \\\hline
\end{tabular}

\begin{solution}[7em]
The inputs are evenly spaced, however there is not a constant ratio between outputs. Rather, we subtract $0.4$ from each output to go to the next. Therefore the function is actually {\it linear} and not exponential.
\end{solution}


\part
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|} \hline
$t$ & $0$ & $3$ & $6$ & $9$ \\\hline
$g(t)$ & $96$ & $24$ & $6$ & $1.5$ \\\hline
\end{tabular}

\begin{solution}[9em]
The inputs are evenly spaced, and there is a constant ratio of $\frac{1}{4}$ between the outputs. {\it To determine this, compute $\frac{24}{96} =\frac{1}{4}$, $\frac{6}{24}=\frac{1}{4}$, and $\frac{1.5}{6} =\frac{1}{4}$.} Therefore the function is exponential.

To find its formula, note that $A=96$ was given. Then $g(t)=96 \cdot b^t$ and we solve for $b$ using another point, say $(3,24)$:
\[
24 = 96\cdot b^3 \quad \implies \quad b^3 = 0.25 \quad \implies \quad b = \sqrt[3]{0.25} = 0.62996 
.\] Therefore the formula is
\[
g(t) = 96 \cdot (0.62996)^t
.\] 
\end{solution}


\part
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|} \hline
$x$ & $0$ & $3$ & $6$ & $9$ & $12$ & $15$ \\\hline
$h(x)$ & $-2$ & $2$ & $-2$ & $-6$ & $-2$ & 2 \\\hline
\end{tabular}

\begin{solution}
The inputs are evenly spaced, however at a first glance this cannot be exponential because we notice that the function is increasing and then decreasing. Exponential functions cannot do this; they always increase or always decrease. So this is {\it not exponential}.
\end{solution}
