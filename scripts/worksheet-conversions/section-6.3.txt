\question Find the doubling time (for increasing quantity) or the half-life (for decreasing quantity).

\begin{parts}
\part $y=150\cdot1.07^t$

\begin{solution}[12em]
The function is increasing, so we compute the doubling time. Setting the function to twice the initial value, then
\[
300 = 150 \cdot (1.07)^t
.\] Dividing by the $150$ yields $2 = (1.07)^t$ and then taking the natural logarithm of both sides gives us
\[
\ln (2) = \ln (1.07^t) \quad \implies \quad \ln (2) = t \ln (1.07)
.\] Therefore the doubling time is
\[
t = \frac{\ln (2)}{\ln (1.07)} = 10.24477 
.\] 
\end{solution}

\part $y=63\cdot0.61^t$

\begin{solution}[12em]
This function is decreasing, so we compute the half-life. Setting the function to half its initial value, then
\[
31.5 = 63 \cdot (0.61)^t
.\] Dividing by $63$ yields $\frac{1}{2} = (0.61)^t$ and then taking the natural logarithm of both sides gives us
\[
\ln (1 /2) = \ln (0.61^t) \quad \implies \quad \ln (1 /2) = t \ln (0.61)
.\] Therefore the half-life is
\[
t = \frac{\ln (1 /2)}{\ln (0.61)}  = 1.40229
.\] 
\end{solution}


\end{parts}

\question An investment begins with $\$2500$ and earns $1.3\%$ per year.

\begin{parts}
\part What is the doubling time?

\begin{solution}[16em]
First, find the formula for the function. Because $A=2500$ and $r=1.3\% = 0.013$, then $b=1+r = 1.013$ and so
\[
y = 2500 (1.013)^t
.\] To find the doubling time of this function, set it equal to twice the initial value. That is,
\[
5000 = 2500 (1.013)^t
.\] Dividing by $2500$ yields $2 = (1.013)^t$. Then take the natural logarithm:
\[
\ln (2) = \ln (1.013^t) \quad \implies \quad \ln (2) = t \ln (1.013)
.\] Therefore the doubling-time is
\[
t = \frac{\ln (2)}{\ln (1.013)}  = 53.66484 \quad \text{years} 
.\] 
\end{solution}

\part How long does it take for the investment to triple?

\begin{solution}[16em]
Now we set the function equal to three times its initial value. That is,
\[
7500 = 2500 (1.013)^t
.\] Dividing by $2500$ yields $3 = (1.013)^t$. Taking the natural logarithm,
\[
\ln (3) = \ln (1.013^t) \quad \implies \quad \ln (3) = t \ln (1.013)
.\] Therefore the time it takes to triple is
\[
t = \frac{\ln (3)}{\ln (1.013)}  = 85.0567  \quad \text{years} 
.\] 
\end{solution}

\end{parts}

\question A radioactive substance decays exponentially. After $t=4$ days, there is $20$mg of the initial amount remaining and after $t=8$ days, there is $12$mg of the initial amount remaining. When will $20\%$ of the initial amount remain?

\begin{solution}[30em]
First, we find the formula for the function. Two points are given: $(4,20)$ and $(8,12)$. Using these, form two equations:
\[
20 = A \cdot b^4 \quad \text{and} \quad 12 = A\cdot b^8
.\] Dividing them we get
\[
\frac{12}{20} = \frac{A\cdot b^8}{A\cdot b^4}  \quad \implies \quad \frac{3}{5} = b^4
\] and so $b=\sqrt[4]{3 /5} = (3 /5)^{1 /4} $. Now, we can update the formula:
\[
y = A \cdot \pbr{\pbr{\frac{3}{5}}^{1 /4}}^t \quad \implies \quad y= A \cdot \pbr{\frac{3}{5}} ^{t /4}
.\] Plugging in a point, say $(4,20)$, then
\[
20 = A \cdot \pbr{\frac{3}{5}}^{4 /4} \quad \implies \quad 20 = A \pbr{\frac{3}{5}}  \quad \implies \quad A = \frac{100}{3}
.\] Now we know that the formula for the function is
\[
y = \frac{100}{3} \cdot \pbr{\frac{3}{5}} ^{t /4}
.\] Now, note that $20\%$ of the original amount is $20\%$ of $\frac{100}{3}$ which is $0.2 \times \frac{100}{3}$. So we set the function equal to this number:
\[
0.2 \pbr{\frac{100}{3}} = \frac{100}{3} \cdot \pbr{\frac{3}{5}} ^{t /4}
.\] Divide both sides by $\frac{100}{3}$:
\[
0.2 = \pbr{\frac{3}{5}} ^{t/4}
.\] Take the natural logarithm of both sides:
\[
\ln (0.2) = \ln \pbr{\pbr{\frac{3}{5}} ^{t/4}} \quad \implies \quad \ln (0.2) = \frac{t}{4} \ln \pbr{\frac{3}{5}} 
.\] Therefore 
\[
t = \frac{4\ln (0.2)}{\ln (3 /5)} = 12.6026 \quad\text{days}  
.\] 
\end{solution}



\question A $24$kg sample of radioactive material loses $3\%$ of its mass every $8$ days.

\begin{parts}
\part What is the half-life?

\begin{solution}[20em]
Here, $A=24$ and $r=-3\% = -0.03$. So $b=1+r = 1+(-0.03) = 0.97$. Also, in the context of section 5.4, $T=8$ days here. Therefore the formula that describes this is
\[
y = A \cdot b^{t /T} = 24 \cdot (0.97)^{t /8}   
.\] To find the half-life, set this function equal to half its initial value which is $12$ here. Therefore,
\[
12 = 24 \cdot (0.97)^{t /8}
.\] Dividing by $24$ and then taking the natural logarithm,
\[
\ln \pbr{\frac{1}{2}} = \ln (0.97^{t /8}) \quad \implies \quad \ln \pbr{\frac{1}{2}}  = \frac{t}{8} \ln (0.97)
.\] Therefore
\[
t = \frac{8 \ln (1 /2)}{\ln (0.97)}  = 182.0528 \quad \text{days} 
.\] 
\end{solution}


\part How long will it take before the sample has shrunk to a weight of $2$ kg?

\begin{solution}[9em]
Using the formula in part (a), we set $y=2$ and solve for $t$. That is,
\[
2 = 24 \pbr{0.97} ^{t /8}
.\] Dividing by $24$ and then taking the natural logarithm,
\[
\ln \pbr{\frac{1}{12}} = \ln (0.97^{t/8}) \quad \implies \quad \ln \pbr{\frac{1}{12}} = \frac{t}{8} \ln (0.97)
.\] Solving for $t$ yields
\[
t = \frac{8\ln (1 /12)}{\ln (0.97)}  = 652.6519 \quad\text{days} 
.\] 
\end{solution}


\end{parts}



\question A rain forest's area is being reduced by $18\%$ every $9$ years. How long does it take for the rain forest to reach one-half of its starting area?

\begin{solution}[20em]
Here we have the growth rate $r=-18\% = -0.18$, so $b=1+r = 0.82$, and $T=9$ years. Therefore the formula that describes this is
\[
y = A \cdot b^{t /T} = A (0.82)^{t /9}
.\] Note that we do not know $A$, nor have enough information to find it. But, it is actually not needed as the following will illustrate.

To find the half-life, set the function to $\frac{1}{2}A$:
\[
\frac{1}{2}A = A (0.82)^{t /9} \quad \implies \quad \frac{1}{2} = (0.82)^{t /9}
\] after dividing by $A$. Taking the natural logarithm,
\[
\ln \pbr{\frac{1}{2}}  = \ln (0.82^{t /9}) \quad \implies \quad \ln \pbr{\frac{1}{2}}  = \frac{t}{9} \ln (0.82)
.\] Solving for $t$, we get
\[
t = \frac{9\ln (1 /2)}{\ln (0.82) } = 31.4351 \quad \text{years} 
.\]  
\end{solution}



\question A population has a continuous growth rate of $12.3\%$. What is the population's doubling time?

\begin{solution}
Given that $k=12.3\% = 0.123$, then the function is
\[
y = A e^{0.123 t}
\] where $A$ is unknown, but is not needed anyway. Setting the formula above to $2A$, then
\[
2A = A e^{0.123t}\quad \implies \quad 2 = e^{0.123 t}
.\] Taking the natural logarithm of both sides yields
\[
\ln (2) = \ln (e^{0.123t}) \quad \implies \quad \ln (2) = 0.123t
.\] Solving for $t$ gives us
\[
t = \frac{\ln (2)}{0.123} = 5.63534 \quad \text{years}
.\]  
\end{solution}
