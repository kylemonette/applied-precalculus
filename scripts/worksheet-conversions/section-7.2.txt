\question Determine what function has been transformed and what transformations have been applied to it. Then graph the function.

\begin{parts}
\part $y=5x^2$

\begin{solution}[5em]
With the original function $f(x)=x^2$, then $y$ is $f(x)$ stretched vertically by a factor of $5$.
\end{solution}

\part $y=\dfrac{1}{7}\sqrt[3]{t}$

\begin{solution}[5em]
With the original function $f(t)=\sqrt[3]{t} $, then $y$ is $f(t)$ compressed vertically by a factor of $7$.
\end{solution}

\part $y=\ln(6x)$

\begin{solution}[5em]
With the original function $f(x)=\ln (x)$, then $y$ is $f(x)$ compressed horizontally by a factor of $6$.
\end{solution}

\part $y=\pbr{\dfrac{1}{5}x}^2$

\begin{solution}[5em]
With the original function $f(x)=x^2$, then $y$ is $f(x)$ stretched horizontally by a factor of $5$.
\end{solution}

\part $y=6\sqrt[5]{x}+8$

\begin{solution}[5em]
With the original function $f(x)=\sqrt[5]{x} $, then $y$ is $f(x)$ stretched vertically by a factor of $6$ and shifted up by $8$.
\end{solution}

\part $y=-\dfrac{1}{4}(x-5)^2$

\begin{solution}[5em]
With the original function $f(x)=x^2$, then $y$ is $f(x)$ compressed vertically by a factor of $4$, shifted right by $5$, and reflected over the $x$-axis.
\end{solution}

\part $y=3e^{\frac{1}{2}x}+1$

\begin{solution}[5em]
With the original function $f(x)=e^x$, then $y$ is $f(x)$ stretched horizontally by a factor of $2$, stretched vertically by a factor of $3$, and shifted up by $1$.
\end{solution}

\part $y=2^{3(x-0.5)}-5$

\begin{solution}[5em]
With the original function $f(x)=2^x$, then $y$ is $f(x)$ shifted right by $0.5$, compressed horizontally by a factor of $3$, and shifted down $5$ units.
\end{solution}
\end{parts}



\question What transformations have been applied to the function $f(x)=13x^2+2x-4$ to obtain $g(x)=26(3x)^2+4(3x)-8$?

\begin{solution}[7em]
Notice that we have the $(3x)$ terms in $g(x)$, suggesting that there is a horizontal scaling. And, the coefficients of $26$, $4$, and $-8$ in $g(x)$ are double those from $f(x)$. This suggests that we did a vertical scaling (i.e., multiply $f(x)$ by $2$ to get $g(x)$). In fact
\[
g(x) \quad = \quad 2 \pbr{13(3x)^2 + 2(3x) - 4}  \quad = \quad 2 f(3x)
.\] Therefore $g$ is $f$ compressed horizontally by a factor of $3$ and stretched vertically by a factor of $2$.
\end{solution}

\question Below is the graph of a transformation of the function $y=x^2$ obtained by scaling. Write a possible formula for it.

\begin{tikzpicture}[scale=0.9]
\begin{axis}[grid=both,
axis lines=middle,
axis line style = very thick,
xmin=-5,xmax=5,
ymin=0,ymax=7,
xtick={-6,...,6},
ytick={-1,1,2,3,4,5,6,7},
minor tick={},
xlabel=\(x\), ylabel=\(y\),
samples=250]
\addplot[domain=-5:5 ,thick] {(0.5*x)^2};

\ifprintanswers
\addplot[domain=-5:5 ,thick,red] {x^2};
\else \fi
\end{axis}
\end{tikzpicture}

\begin{solution}[6em]
In the graph above, we plot in red $y=x^2$. Notice that for $y=x^2$ then we have the point $(1,1)$ but for the black function we have $(2,1)$. Similarly, $(2,4)$ on $y=x^2$ corresponds with $(4,4)$ for the black function.

That is, if the $y$-values are kept constant, then the $x$-values are being multiplied by $2$. Therefore, the function in black is a horizontal scaling of $y=x^2$, namely a horizontal stretching by a factor of $2$.
So, we have $y=\pbr{\frac{1}{2}x} ^2$ for the formula.

{\it We remark that you could interpret this as a vertical scaling instead of a horizontal one. Here, you would consider constant $x$-values and see what happens to the $y$-values. For example, the point $(2,4)$ on the $y=x^2$ function aligns with $(2,1)$ on the black function. Because $1$ is one-fourth of $4$, then there is a vertical compression by a factor of $4$, and therefore $y=\frac{1}{4}x^2$ is the formula. Notice that this is {\bf exactly} the same formula as we got with the ``horizontal scaling'' approach, if you simplify that formula.} 
\end{solution}



\question A function $g(x)$ is formed by reflecting the function $f(x)$ over the $y$-axis, stretching it vertically by a factor of $5$, and shifting it down $6$. Write a formula for $g$ in terms of $f$.

\begin{solution}[6em]
Here we have $g(x)=5f(-x)-6$.
\end{solution}


\question A function $g(x)$ is formed by shifting the function $f(x)$ right $0.6$, stretching it horizontally by a factor of $3$, compressing it vertically by a factor of $2$, and shifting it up $14$. Write a formula for $g$ in terms of $f$.

\begin{solution}
Here we have
\[
g(x) = \frac{1}{2} f\pbr{\frac{1}{3}(x-0.6)} +14
.\] 
\end{solution}
