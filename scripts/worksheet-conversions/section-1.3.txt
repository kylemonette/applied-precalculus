

\question Use the table to find each of the following.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|} \hline
$t$ & $-5$ & $0$ & $5$ & $10$ \\\hline
$g(t)$ & $9$ & $-1$ & $9$ & $5$ \\\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{parts}
\part $g(0)$

\part $g(5)$

\part The value(s) of $t$ for which $g(t)=9$.

\begin{solution}[7em]
From the table, $g(0)=-1$, $g(5)=9$, and lastly $t=-5$ and $t=5$ both satisfy $g(t)=9$.
\end{solution}


\end{parts}

\question Guess a formula for the function $h(t)$ given numerically in the table below. Fill in the missing values.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline
$t$ & $-2$ & $-1$ & $0$ & $1$ & $2$ & $3$ & $4$ & $5$ & $6$ \\\hline
$h(t)$ & $4$ & $2$ & $0$ & $-2$ & $-4$ & $-6$ & $-8$ & \hspace{2em} & \hspace{2em} \\\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{solution}[10em]
Notice that the inputs $t$ go up by $1$ each time. And, the outputs $h(t)$ go down by $2$ each time. Or, you can think of the outputs as being the inputs multiplied by $-2$.

Therefore, $h(t)=-2t$. Thus $h(5)=-10$ and $h(6)=-12$.
\end{solution}

\question A driver of an SUV fills up his gas tank and starts a highway trip to a faraway city. Let $G$, in gallons, be the amount of gas left in the tank after driving $d$ miles. Of course $G$ is a function of $d$; that is, $G=G(d)$. Here is partial data about the function:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|}\hline
$d$ & $0$ & $60$ & $120$ & $180$ & $240$ & $300$ & $360$ & $420$ & $480$ & $510$ \\\hline
$G(d)$ & $17$ & $15$  & $13$ & $11$ & $9$ & $7$ & \ifprintanswers \textcolor{red}{5} \else \hspace{3em} \fi & \ifprintanswers \textcolor{red}{3} \else \hspace{3em} \fi & \ifprintanswers \textcolor{red}{1} \else \hspace{3em} \fi & \ifprintanswers \textcolor{red}{0} \else \hspace{3em} \fi \\\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Find gas mileage of the SUV; that is, the number of miles the SUV gets per gallon. Fill in the missing numbers.

\begin{solution}
Except for the last column when $d=510$, note that the miles $d$ go up by $60$ each time, and the gallons $G(d)$ go down by $2$ each time. Using these rules, the completed table is given above.

Therefore, the car can drive $510$ miles using $17$ gallons of gas. Thus, the gas mileage is
\[
\frac{510 \text{ miles} }{17 \text{ gallons} } \;=\; 30 \frac{\text{miles} }{\text{gallon} }  
.\] 
\end{solution}
